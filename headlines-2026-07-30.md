## Note

Selection window: 2026-07-29T13:36 UTC through 2026-07-30T13:36 UTC (09:36 ET to 09:36 ET). All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool; items were filtered on `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time. Several WSJ links carried `?mod=...` tracking parameters in the RSS feed, which were stripped for clean canonical URLs. One WSJ selection (the Fed rate decision) was sourced from WSJ's "social economy" feed, whose `title` field is a conversational sentence rather than a formatted headline; the published headline text could not be independently confirmed because wsj.com is paywalled and unfetchable from this environment, so the headline was reconstructed from the article's own URL slug (`fed-holds-rates-steady-but-three-officials-voted-for-increase`) rather than guessed freely.

AP does not publish a public RSS feed. Its candidate pool (100 items) was built from Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed, whose links are obfuscated `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirects. These were resolved to canonical `apnews.com` URLs, and each candidate's original publication time was independently verified against the article's own JSON-LD `datePublished` metadata (the authoritative source, not Google News's `pubDate`). This surfaced a discrepancy: Google News listed "Fed leaves interest rate unchanged but with 3 dissents" with a `pubDate` of 2026-07-29T21:18 UTC (inside the window), but the article's own `datePublished` was 2026-07-29T04:01 UTC — more than nine hours before the window opened. The similarly-dated "Oil prices jump, while the Dow drops more than 1,100" story was checked as a possible substitute and also predated the window (`datePublished` 2026-07-29T03:43 UTC). Both were excluded on that basis and replaced with "EU lays out $11.4 billion for 7 AI gigafactories," verified in-window at 2026-07-30T10:01 UTC.

The dominant story of the cycle was the direct U.S.-Iran military exchange — the U.S. retaliating for Tuesday's Iranian attack on American forces, with the conflict spreading to new fronts (Egypt struck by drone, per WSJ; Saudi Arabia formally entering the fight, per NYT) — selected by all four outlets.
