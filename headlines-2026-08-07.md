## Note

Selection window: 2026-08-06T13:48 UTC through 2026-08-07T13:48 UTC (09:48 ET to 09:48 ET). All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool (365, 316, and 186 raw items respectively); items were filtered on each item's own `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time, leaving 99 WSJ, 107 NYT, and 33 NBC in-window candidates after deduplication by canonical URL. For NYT specifically, several items carried in-window `pubDate`s but URL-embedded original-publish dates from two or more calendar days earlier (e.g., an AI-data-center-pollution piece dated 08/05, a SpaceX-moon-crash piece dated 08/05, a Cambridge-plagiarism piece dated 08/03); these were treated as republished/updated older stories, not new developments, and excluded from consideration even though their feed timestamps fell inside the window.

Two access limitations affected verification and, for AP, link selection. First, wsj.com and nytimes.com article pages were not fetched directly this run — both returned fetch errors consistent with bot detection — so WSJ and NYT selections rely on their own RSS `title`/`link`/`pubDate` fields. Two WSJ items (the Blanche-confirmation and birthright-citizenship entries) carried sentence-style, non-headline RSS titles; in both cases a proper headline was reconstructed directly from the article's own URL slug rather than fabricated. Second, apnews.com's robots.txt explicitly disallows Claude-family crawlers (`ClaudeBot`, `anthropic-ai`), so no apnews.com pages were fetched. AP does not publish a general-news RSS feed, so its 100-item candidate pool was built from Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed (all 100 items fell inside the window per Google's reported timestamps). Rather than link to apnews.com or an unresolved `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect, each AP-sourced headline below links to an independent wire-copy mirror (KSAT-TV, Yahoo News, WFMZ's AP wire section, and NPR), each confirmed via direct fetch (or, for WFMZ, via its dedicated `/news/ap/` wire-syndication URL path) to carry an explicit Associated Press byline or named-AP-reporter/source attribution matching the Google News-indexed headline and details. This is a deliberate substitution, not a fabricated or guessed URL.
