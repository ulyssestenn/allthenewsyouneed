Each finalist's redirect page was fetched to extract its embedded article id/timestamp/signature triple, which was then exchanged against Google News's internal decode endpoint to recover the canonical `apnews.com` URL. The resulting five URLs' slugs are semantically consistent with their headlines and publication order; apnews.com's own bot protection prevented a final direct-fetch confirmation.
- Two candidates were dropped after cross-checking against yesterday's digest (2026-09-14) because they were the identical story/link already published there: WSJ's "Chip Stocks Tumble After AI Leaders Call for Slowdown in Development" (replaced with "China Tightens Exit Restrictions as Tech Race With U.S. Intensifies") and NYT's "Saudi Arabia Faces 'Worst-Case Scenario' After Iran's Attacks and Trump's Rebuffs" (replaced with the same day's fresher, distinct follow-on story "Gulf Arabs Seek Options After U.S. Bases Did Not Deter Iran").

**Selection rationale:** Two developments dominated the cycle across all four outlets. First, the Supreme Court's rejection of the Trump administration's attempt to impose new mail-ballot screening rules ahead of the midterms — a durable constitutional and electoral-administration story that each outlet covered independently (WSJ, NYT, NBC, AP all lead or near-lead with it). Second, the fallout from AI industry leaders' public call to slow development, which fractured into genuinely distinct threads rather than one repeated story: a market shock as tech/chip stocks sold off (NYT), Trump's public dismissal of AI safety concerns as a "hoax" (NBC), and the muted, election-focused response from Congress (AP) — captured once each, by different outlets, as separate durable consequences. A second cross-outlet thread was the EPA's repeal of power-plant greenhouse-gas limits, a major and consequential climate-regulation rollback picked up independently by WSJ, NBC, and AP. A third thread was security and energy fallout from the Iran/Houthi-Saudi conflict, reflected in NYT's Gulf-states story and continuing Fed/Treasury market pressure. Individual outlets also captured distinct institutional stories not shared elsewhere: the Bank of Japan's rate-hike trajectory and China's tightened exit controls (WSJ); the U.S.'s disclosed deployment of weapons in space and the Fed chair-designate's looming clash with Trump (NYT); the Pentagon inspector general's finding of a munitions shortfall from the Iran war and DHS's own watchdog report on detainee conditions at "Alligator Alcatraz" (NBC); and NATO fighters shooting down a drone over Lithuania, a significant escalation in the alliance's direct engagement near the Russia-Ukraine war (AP).
