**Selection window:** Stories originally published between 2026-11-13 13:39 UTC and 2026-11-14 13:39 UTC, verified against each feed's `pubDate` (not update time or retrieval time).

**Access and feed limitations:** All WSJ (Dow Jones), NYT, and NBC RSS feeds listed in the sourcing instructions were reachable directly. AP has no general-purpose public RSS feed, so candidates were sourced via Google News' `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed, as instructed. The five Google News redirect links corresponding to the AP picks below were resolved to their canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URLs using a headless browser to follow the actual client-side redirect (confirmed HTTP 200 on each), so the AP links in this digest are verified canonical URLs, not guessed ones.

**Selection rationale:** Four developments dominated the news cycle and were weighed carefully against the "durable significance" test rather than included reflexively: (1) Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's forced resignation amid a Chinese espionage investigation, which bears on AI/chip export policy and U.S.-China tech competition; (2) a nationwide FAA ground stop from an air-traffic-control communications failure, which stranded flights across the country and raised infrastructure/regulatory questions; (3) a worldwide Microsoft outage disrupting Outlook/Azure-dependent businesses and governments; and (4) Trump's planned three-way meeting with Xi and Putin at APEC, with nuclear proliferation reportedly on the agenda. Each outlet's five selections include at most one headline tied to the Nvidia/Huang story and at most one tied to the Microsoft outage, so as not to let a single news cycle crowd out other durable developments (public health, courts, war). Tuesday's U.S. midterm election results (Alaska's first Democratic governor since 1998, Lauren Boebert's defeat, a Mississippi Senate upset) were considered but mostly excluded as individual-race coverage without a clearly distinct 30-day consequence beyond the already-reported shift in control; the Supreme Court's asylum case was preferred instead as a concrete, pending constitutional question.
