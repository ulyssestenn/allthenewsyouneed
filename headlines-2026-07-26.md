## Note

Selection window: 2026-07-25T13:36 UTC through 2026-07-26T13:36 UTC. All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool; items were filtered on `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time. WSJ article pages return a bot-protection placeholder to automated requests without a browser session, so WSJ links could not be live-verified by fetching the article page directly; they are the exact `<link>` values published in WSJ's own RSS feeds, with the one tracking query parameter present (`?mod=rss_Technology` on the AI-biological-weapons story) stripped for a clean canonical URL.

AP does not publish a public RSS feed. Its candidate pool was first built from Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed (HTTP 200, 100 items), but as on prior days that feed's `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` links resolve into a client-side Google interstitial rather than the canonical apnews.com article, so they could not serve as final URLs, and a large share of the Google News items were topic-hub pages (e.g., "Russia - AP News," "Trials - AP News") or routine box scores rather than articles. Instead, AP's homepage was fetched directly and its embedded `schema.org` `mainEntity.itemListElement` structured data (AP's own ranked list of current homepage article placements) supplied the candidate pool, and `apnews.com/search` was used to locate additional slugs for topics of interest. Every AP candidate was confirmed by fetching the live apnews.com article page and reading its `datePublished` metadata directly, rather than trusting Google News' feed-retrieval timestamp — this caught several Google-News items whose feed pubDate fell inside the window but whose true `datePublished` did not: "Yemen's Houthis target Saudi Arabia in response to strikes on Hodeida city" (true `datePublished` 2026-07-25T09:53:27Z, before window open), "Bridges and infrastructure hit as US struck deeper inside Iran" (true `datePublished` 2026-07-24T19:59:14Z, and an exact-URL repeat of yesterday's AP selection), and "Fire looms near Oregon town as fourth firefighter dies weeks after Colorado blaze" (true `datePublished` 2026-07-25T13:31:06Z, five minutes before window open). All three were excluded on that basis.

NBC's in-window pool was unusually thin: only 11 items across all eight section feeds fell inside the 24-hour window, of which several were shopping filler (an Amazon Lego sale), crime-of-the-day or novelty items (an influencer's killing, a video of a man kicking a sea lion, a Comic-Con appearance by Rick Moranis), a personality-driven sports item (Caitlin Clark), and one horse-race politics item (a Kornacki segment on which states Democrats "have to win"), all of which were deprioritized per the selection standard. That left exactly five qualifying items, two of which are NBC/Today video segments rather than text articles (the Meet the Press interview with Amb. Mike Waltz and the Today segment on Trump holding off on Iran War expansion) — these are included as-is because they are NBC's own published items on genuinely significant developments and no stronger text-article alternative existed in the window.

The dominant story of the cycle was the van-ramming attack at Berlin's Pride festival, treated by all four outlets as suspected terrorism and still under active manhunt as of window close, with NBC additionally reporting the suspect's "Islamist" ties. A second major thread was the Iran war's continued fragile pause, with both NYT and AP describing a second straight day without strikes even as talks remain uncertain, while a related storyline broke out around Trump's push for nuclear energy deals (including with Saudi Arabia) and possible family financial benefit, corroborated independently by NYT, AP (a separate story on nuclear regulators moving to eliminate a radiation-exposure safety rule), and NBC (Amb. Waltz's on-record comments about the unimplemented US-Saudi nuclear deal).
