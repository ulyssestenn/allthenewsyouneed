## Note

**Selection window:** 2026-08-25 13:40 UTC to 2026-08-26 13:40 UTC (24 hours), filtered by original publication timestamp (each outlet's RSS `<pubDate>`, or each shortlisted AP article's own `datePublished`/`dateModified` JSON-LD page metadata), not update or retrieval time.

**Access notes:**
- All eight listed WSJ feeds, all ten listed NYT feeds, and all eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly and parsed successfully (920 raw items across the three outlets; 368 fell inside the 24-hour window before deduplication, 265 after). No domain or feed was inaccessible.
- AP does not publish a general RSS feed. The Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`) was fetched for candidate discovery (100 items). Its article links are opaque `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect tokens; following them (`curl -L`) lands on a Google-rendered interstitial page that resolves the true destination client-side via JavaScript rather than exposing it in the HTML, so they cannot be resolved to `apnews.com` by direct request. Each shortlisted AP candidate was instead matched by headline and timing to its canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL by browsing AP's own topic hub pages (`apnews.com/world-news`, `/business`, `/us-news`, `/hub/syria`, `/hub/nepal`), then confirming the match against that page's own `datePublished`/`dateModified` JSON-LD metadata. AP's on-site search (`apnews.com/search`) returned a generic, query-independent result set and was not usable for this. One candidate — a PCE inflation-gauge story surfaced by Google News at 12:42 UTC — could not be located on AP's own site under a matching headline or timestamp within the window and was dropped in favor of AP's same-morning, verifiably in-window GDP report, which covers substantially the same economic-data news cycle.
- WSJ and NYT article pages return 401/403 to non-browser HTTP requests even though their RSS feeds are open, so WSJ/NYT links above are used verbatim from each outlet's own RSS `<link>` field (WSJ links had tracking query parameters, e.g. `?mod=rss_Technology`, stripped to their canonical form), consistent with each feed's own headline text.
- NBC's own RSS feeds carried real reporting on most of today's top stories but skewed toward video segments for a few (e.g., its first Pennsylvania-measles and Dolly Parton items were video posts); in both cases a same-day NBC text article covering the identical news was available and used instead.

**Selection rationale:** Two hard regulatory/economic developments led the window across outlets: Meta's roughly $16-18 billion multistate settlement over child social-media-addiction claims (WSJ, NYT, NBC) is a precedent-setting resolution of the largest tech child-safety litigation to date, and the U.S.-Canada trade war's escalation into mutual tariffs (WSJ, NYT, NBC) marks a genuine rupture between historically close trading partners with direct consequences for consumers and industry on both sides of the border. Geopolitical realignment also advanced on two distinct fronts: the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces' formal dissolution into the Syrian army (NYT, AP) consolidates Damascus's post-Assad authority over the country's northeast, while Oman-Iran talks raising hope for a Strait of Hormuz reopening (WSJ) and a first-of-its-kind claimed cyberattack by a pro-Russian hacker group on Norway's national digital infrastructure (AP) each represent discrete security developments with durable follow-on consequences. Economic data releases (the Fed's preferred PCE inflation gauge on WSJ, Q2 GDP on AP) matter directly to the Federal Reserve's rate decision at the approaching Jackson Hole meeting. A major peer-reviewed study finding at least 1 in 4 NFL players develop CTE (NYT) is a landmark public-health finding with durable labor and regulatory implications for professional football. A federal judge striking down Texas's ban on drag shows (NBC) and Pennsylvania's first measles deaths of the year (NBC) are, respectively, a substantive civil-liberties ruling and a public-health milestone in an ongoing vaccine-preventable outbreak. ICE arrests hitting a record monthly high under the second Trump administration (AP) is a hard data point central to the year's defining immigration-enforcement story, and a Himalayan avalanche-triggered flash flood leaving hundreds missing in Nepal (AP) is an acute, still-unfolding humanitarian disaster. Dolly Parton's death at 80 (WSJ, NYT, NBC) was selected once per outlet despite its volume of coverage: as one of the most widely mourned American cultural figures of the era, with a five-decade catalog and philanthropic legacy that will be revisited for years, it clears the standard's bar for a culture story of "exceptional scale" and "lasting change in ... shared culture" rather than being included for viral or sentimental reasons. Deprioritized across all four outlets: South Carolina, Oklahoma, Alaska, and Georgia primary-runoff results (routine electoral horse-race coverage, notwithstanding real interest in one candidate succeeding her late brother in the Senate), the Chucky-mask arrest and Capitol guillotine stories (crime-of-the-day), Target's Halloween-costume backlash (social-media outrage/lifestyle filler), and NFL season-preview and U.S. Open recap content (previews/routine sports coverage).
