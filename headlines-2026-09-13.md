- All five NBC links were verified to return HTTP 200.
- **apnews.com was completely inaccessible this cycle** — direct fetches (curl with the specified user agent, a full browser user agent, and the WebFetch tool) all returned HTTP 403 for the homepage, topic pages, and individual article URLs. As instructed for domains that cannot be fetched directly, AP candidates were identified from the specified Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`), but Google's RSS `<link>` values are obfuscated `news.google.com` redirect pages that resolve only via client-side JavaScript, not a plain HTTP redirect curl can follow. Each finalist's exact wire headline, byline framing, and content were therefore independently confirmed via web search and linked to a verified AP wire-syndication partner page (a broadcaster or newspaper site carrying the unmodified AP wire text) that returned HTTP 200 — bangordailynews.com, local10.com (used twice, for two distinct AP wire stories), dailygazette.com, and news4jax.com. All five AP links carry headline text identical to AP's own and publication content falling inside the selection window.

**Selection rationale:** The single dominant development of the cycle, picked up independently by all four outlets, was Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's public call for the AI industry to slow development to let safety measures catch up — WSJ led with the corporate-rivalry angle ("Biggest AI Rivals Agree"), NYT and AP covered Amodei's own statement and reasoning directly, and NBC covered both the CEO consensus and a related Sunday-show interview with an AI whistleblower calling for global regulatory coordination. A second cross-outlet thread was continued Iran-war escalation: an Iranian commercial ship was struck near the Strait of Hormuz with one dead (AP), while NYT's reporting explained the escalation's origin in Iranian hard-liners sabotaging an earlier peace deal with Trump. A third thread concerned federal institutional guardrails: a federal judge's ruling that the Trump administration's plan to cut FEMA staffing by half was unlawful was independently selected by both NYT and AP. Beyond these threads, outlets diverged to capture other durable stories: a $20 billion J&J-to-Apollo medical-device divestiture and Larry Ellison's abrupt reversal of a $7.5 billion Oracle stock sale (WSJ, business/markets); Mark Carney's bid to make Canada an EU "associate member" after U.S. trade talks collapsed, and record crypto-industry donations to the UK's Reform Party (WSJ, geopolitics/political finance); Trump's use of executive power to curb speech, and mounting death and destruction from Himalayan flooding in Nepal (NYT); the Treasury secretary's losing standoff with bond markets, and House Speaker Johnson's remarks on both promised $5,000 checks and a hoped-for end to the Iran war after the midterms (NBC); and a BRICS summit joint statement condemning unilateral sanctions and voicing concern over Middle East strikes, alongside an AP data analysis finding the Justice Department's felony-assault prosecutions of protesters mostly failing in court (AP).
