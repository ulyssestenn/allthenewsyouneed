## Note

Selection window: 2026-07-24T13:36 UTC through 2026-07-25T13:36 UTC. All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool; items were filtered on `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time. AP does not publish a public RSS feed. Its candidates were first sourced via Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed (HTTP 200, 99 in-window items), but as on prior days that feed's `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` links resolve into a client-side Google interstitial (confirmed HTTP 200 with no redirect via `curl -IL`) rather than the canonical apnews.com article, so they could not serve as final URLs. Instead, AP's homepage was fetched directly and its embedded `schema.org` `ItemList` structured data (a ranked list of AP's current homepage placements) supplied initial candidates; additional candidates surfaced only in the Google News pool were cross-verified by querying AP's own site-search pages (`apnews.com/search?q=...`) for matching slugs. Every AP selection below was confirmed by fetching the live apnews.com article page and reading its `datePublished` (and, where the story is a live wire update, `dateModified`) metadata directly, rather than trusting Google News' feed-retrieval timestamp. This caught one Google News item, "New US tariffs linked to foreign forced labor claims anger trading partners," whose feed pubDate (07-24 19:13 UTC) was inside the window but whose true `datePublished` on apnews.com was 2026-07-24T07:03:22Z — outside the window — so it was excluded. WSJ article pages return a bot-protection placeholder to automated requests without a browser session, so WSJ links could not be live-verified by fetching the article page directly; they are the exact `<link>` values published in WSJ's own RSS feeds, with WSJ's feed-tracking query parameters (`?mod=...`) stripped for a clean canonical URL. One WSJ item's headline ("Taylor Farms Sought to Delay Its Cyclospora Recall Announcement") was reconstructed from the article's URL slug and RSS description, since the feed's `<title>` field contained a caption-style lede sentence rather than a formal headline. One WSJ item ("America and Iran Are Locked in a Cycle of Escalation," a "What's News" newsletter roundup published later at a `pls_whats_news` tracking URL) was treated as a duplicate of the dedicated analysis article "The U.S. and Iran Are Stuck in a Cycle of Military Escalation" on the same topic and excluded in favor of the latter. One NYT item ("China Wields Its Rare Earth Leverage Over Europe With New Export Controls") was an exact-URL repeat of an item already selected in yesterday's (2026-07-24) digest and was excluded as continuing coverage rather than a fresh development.
