The Nepal-Tibet flood disaster, with its death toll now past 900 and thousands still missing or unaccounted for, was the second-most-selected story, again with each outlet choosing a distinct angle: WSJ on the dangerous tunnel rescues as the toll rose past 900, NYT and AP (independently) on a school principal's evacuation of 900 students before his school was destroyed, and NBC on the milestone death-toll and missing-persons count. The Grand Canyon flash flood — one confirmed dead, roughly 15 people still missing days later — was independently selected by both NBC and AP. Beyond these: WSJ's reporting on Japan's record defense-spending request amid rising China pressure, and on two AI-infrastructure-adjacent stories (SoftBank's multibillion-dollar bid to land OpenAI as a data-center tenant, and G20 regulators' formal warning about AI-driven financial-stability risk); NYT's investigation into mail ballots arriving late and being discarded ahead of the midterms, a concrete governance consequence of the Postal Service rule change reported the day before; NBC's report on the House returning to Washington with government-funding negotiations as its top priority, and its report on bootleg "Spider-Man" screenings circumventing Hollywood's Russia boycott, a sanctions-evasion and film-distribution story; and AP's reporting on Congo's Ebola outbreak surpassing 6,000 confirmed cases, a major and underrecognized public-health crisis, and on Israel and Greece signing a multi-billion-dollar weapons deal.
