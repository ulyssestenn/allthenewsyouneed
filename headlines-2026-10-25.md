## Note

**Selection window:** Stories originally published between 2026-10-24 13:39 UTC and 2026-10-25 13:39 UTC. Items were filtered by original pubDate (not update or retrieval time); substantially identical stories and canonical URLs were deduplicated before ranking.

**Access and feed limitations:**
- All eight WSJ feeds, ten NYT feeds, and eight NBC feeds fetched successfully with the specified browser-style user agent, yielding roughly 370 in-window candidate items combined.
- AP publishes no general-purpose RSS feed. Per instructions, candidates were drawn from `news.google.com/rss/search?q=site:apnews.com+when:1d`, which returned 100 in-window items after dedup to 88. `apnews.com` itself returns HTTP 403 (Cloudflare interstitial) to both direct requests and a rendered headless-browser fetch, and Google's redirect links (`news.google.com/rss/articles/...`) do not resolve via a normal HTTP follow — the destination is embedded as an opaque signed token rather than a plaintext URL. All five AP canonical links above were obtained by replicating Google News' own internal article-resolution request (the signed `data-n-a-id` / `data-n-a-ts` / `data-n-a-sg` token served with each article page, submitted to Google's `batchexecute` endpoint), which returns the live `apnews.com/article/...` destination directly from Google; each resolved slug was checked against its source headline for topical consistency before use.
- Two items were dropped after detecting they were not actually new: a WSJ "France" item today shared its exact URL with yesterday's (2026-10-24) WSJ #1 selection — the article had simply been re-timestamped by an ongoing live update, not newly published; and an NBC Nightly News video clip reprising "Iranian plots … advanced stages" was a same-day rebroadcast of the story already published and selected yesterday, not a new development. Both were excluded. A third, AP's "2 ex-FBI officials charged with lying about involvement in investigation into Hunter Biden's laptop," carried an in-window index timestamp today but is the identical story (same charges, same headline) already published and used in yesterday's digest; it was excluded as a non-original repeat rather than treated as newly published.

**Principal selection rationale:** Selection favored developments with durable, 30-day-plus consequences over campaign-trail theater, crime-of-the-day coverage, and sports/entertainment, all of which made up a large share of raw candidate volume (especially from NBC and the AP/Google News pool). Priority went to acute security escalation (the U.S. evacuating Mideast outposts over an Iranian plot, and renewed fears of direct U.S.-Iran conflict); concluded or advancing institutional and legal matters (the New York Archdiocese's finalized $800M abuse settlement, charges tied to a federal espionage probe at an AI lab, the EPA's rollback of chemical-pollution rules); election governance and integrity fights playing out in real time (the Alaska Senate certification dispute, California's standoff with the DOJ over ballot data, Brazil's runoff with direct bearing on U.S.-Latin America relations); and structural technology and infrastructure stories with lasting consequences (U.S. allies' exposure to SpaceX/Starlink dependency, the emerging U.S.-China split over AI governance, Kyiv's wartime power grid). Ongoing French civil unrest and Mideast security developments (Saudi airport reopening, West Bank operations) were included for their continuing, concrete consequences rather than as previews of scheduled events.

**Strongest excluded candidate per outlet:**
- **WSJ:** "Kim Jong Un Makes Surprise Trip to China, Crosses New Bridge" — a genuine diplomatic signal, but a routine, largely symbolic visit judged less consequential than the five selected items.
- **NYT:** "Audi to Build Factory in Georgia, Trump Says" — a solid trade/manufacturing story, but excluded in NYT's own list since the same development is already well covered via WSJ's selection, in favor of NYT's distinct environmental-policy and France pieces.
- **NBC:** "12 injured, including several minors, in shooting near Florida A&M University's stadium" — a large-scale incident, but excluded as crime-of-the-day coverage per the selection standard, despite its scale.
- **AP:** "More than 400,000 protest across France, challenging Macron as Paris clashes erupt" — a major unrest story, narrowly excluded because France unrest is already well represented across WSJ and NBC's selections, in favor of giving AP's own five more topical breadth (elections, law, tech policy).

**Redundancy check:** No outlet's final five had three or more selections concerning the same single underlying development. Mideast security/conflict appears twice in WSJ (Iran evacuation; and indirectly via the Saudi espionage case) and twice in NBC (Starlink/Ukraine war; West Bank), and the Alaska Senate certification dispute appears in both NYT and NBC — but in each case these are distinct underlying developments (different countries, actors, or triggering events) rather than repeated coverage of one story.
