**Selection window:** Stories originally published between 2026-11-18 14:39 UTC and 2026-11-19 14:39 UTC, verified against each feed's `pubDate` (not update time or retrieval time).

**Access and feed limitations:** All eight WSJ (Dow Jones) feeds, all ten NYT feeds, and all eight NBC feeds were reachable and returned full, current item sets. AP has no general-purpose public RSS feed, so candidates were sourced via Google News' `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed, as instructed. Direct requests to apnews.com pages return a Cloudflare bot-verification challenge, so AP headline text and publication times are taken from Google News' indexed metadata. Each selected story's Google News redirect link was resolved to its canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL with a headless browser (the client-side redirect completes even though the destination page's full Cloudflare challenge render times out), so all five AP links above are genuine canonical AP article URLs rather than Google News passthrough links. One early AP candidate ("Mideast nations ramp up efforts to avert all-out war as Israel urges Iran strikes") was discarded after its resolved canonical URL turned out to be the same `apnews.com/article/iran-us-kuwait-gulf-attacks-bahrain-...` article already featured in yesterday's digest under a different Google News snippet/title — i.e., not a new original story — and was replaced with the Fed minutes story.

**Selection rationale:** Four developments dominated the day across outlets independently: (1) the UN's International Court of Justice ruling, for the first time in its history, that a state (Myanmar) is legally responsible for genocide, against the Rohingya — a landmark precedent in international law; (2) the first-ever three-way Trump-Xi-Putin summit in China, framed by WSJ as the U.S. formally entering an unprecedented two-front nuclear-power rivalry, and by NYT as Xi and Putin jointly working to erode U.S. global leverage, with no major breakthroughs on arms control; (3) Anthropic's Wall Street debut — the largest IPO in history, pricing at $83/share for an eventual valuation north of $1 trillion (WSJ and AP cite figures as high as $1.5 trillion), with durable consequences for AI-industry financing, corporate governance, and Bay Area wealth concentration; and (4) House Democrats' nomination of Hakeem Jeffries for speaker, positioning him to become the first Black Speaker of the House if Democrats take the majority, a historic institutional milestone. Beyond these four, each outlet's fifth slot went to its strongest remaining distinct story: for WSJ, a Houthi missile strike that damaged Saudi Arabia's tallest tower (housing its sovereign-wealth fund), widening the regional war beyond Gulf shipping; for NYT, a rare Israeli tank incursion across the Gaza cease-fire line, testing the truce's durability; for NBC, a Russian-national espionage case targeting Palantir employees, a national-security story with no comparable alternative in NBC's eight feeds (NBC's feeds, unlike the other three outlets, carried no Myanmar coverage); for AP, the Fed's November minutes signaling more rate increases are needed despite the latest hold.
