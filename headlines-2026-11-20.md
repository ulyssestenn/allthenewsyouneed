## Note

**Selection window:** Stories originally published between 2026-11-19 14:39 UTC and 2026-11-20 14:39 UTC, verified against each feed's `pubDate` (not update time or retrieval time).

**Access and feed limitations:** All eight WSJ (Dow Jones) feeds, all ten NYT feeds, and all eight NBC feeds were reachable and returned full, current item sets. AP has no general-purpose public RSS feed, so candidates were sourced via Google News' `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed, as instructed. Direct requests to apnews.com — both plain HTTP requests and a headless browser — return a Cloudflare bot-verification challenge ("Just a moment...") that does not clear, so AP headline text and publication times are taken from Google News' indexed metadata. Each selected story's Google News redirect link (the full link including its trailing encoded continuation and `?oc=5` parameter — a truncated form of the same link returns an HTTP 400 from Google's resolver) was resolved to its canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL with a headless browser, which completes the client-side redirect before the destination's Cloudflare challenge renders. One candidate headline, "Poland says a Russian missile that crashed in its territory was a provocation," resolved to a generic rolling Ukraine-war liveblog URL rather than a dedicated article, so the more specific, properly-resolving duplicate ("Russian missile's crash in Poland was likely an intentional provocation, Polish prime minister says") was used instead. One NBC feed item ("White House National Cyber Director Departs Amid Tech Outages" in the general News feed) linked to an unrelated Today.com video; the equivalent, correctly-linked article from NBC's Politics feed was substituted.

**Selection rationale:** Four developments recurred across outlets independently: (1) the conclusion of the first-ever trilateral Trump-Xi-Putin summit at APEC — framed by WSJ as ending with a joint denunciation of nuclear war and by NYT as Putin claiming a symbolic win while sharing "values" with Trump, with no substantive arms-control breakthroughs; (2) the Senate's passage of a major bipartisan permitting-reform bill to speed energy, grid and data-center projects, covered by NYT, NBC and AP; (3) a major financial-sector data breach spanning Visa, Mastercard, JPMorgan and Chase customer records, covered by WSJ, NYT and AP; and (4) the market aftermath of Anthropic's IPO, as a global AI-stock selloff overshadowed the debut itself, covered by WSJ, NBC and AP. Beyond these, each outlet's remaining slots went to its strongest distinct stories: for WSJ, the U.S. floating NATO membership for Ukraine (a potentially consequential shift in the war's trajectory) and P&G's plan to cut up to 30% of its product-supply workforce to automation; for NYT, Britain and the EU's Brexit-reset deal and Nvidia being asked to supply chips to firms tied to the Chinese military; for NBC, deadly Russian strikes on Kyiv's bridges hours after the summit and a still-unresolved outage of a classified NSA network; for AP, a Russian missile crash in Poland that Warsaw called a likely intentional provocation, and California's attorney general subpoenaing Nvidia. Stories whose significance depended on novelty, lifestyle content, or scheduled-event coverage — holiday gift-book roundups, Cincinnati Zoo's hippo Fiona, Grand Theft Auto VI launch coverage, CMA Awards recaps — were deprioritized throughout.

**Strongest excluded candidate per outlet:**
- **WSJ:** "Senate Passes Measure Designed to Speed Up Energy Projects, Lower Consumer Costs" — a genuine policy story, but judged less distinctively significant for WSJ's list than the financial-data breach, the AI-stock selloff and P&G's automation-driven layoffs, and already well-represented by NYT, NBC and AP.
- **NYT:** "New Ph.D. Enrollment in the Sciences Dropped 10 Percent Under Trump" — a real, durable institutional story about the U.S. science pipeline, but judged less immediately consequential than the five chosen.
- **NBC:** "Justice Department Seeks Dismissal of Its Lawsuit in Alaska Senate Race" — a real institutional/electoral story affecting Senate composition, but narrower in durable consequence than the NSA network outage.
- **AP:** "Kushner Says He Believes Russia Is 'Comfortable' With NATO-Like Security Guarantees for Ukraine" — a notable diplomatic signal, but a secondhand characterization rather than a concrete event, judged less durable than the Poland missile incident.

**Redundancy check:** No single outlet's final five contains three or more headlines about the same underlying development. Across outlets, four developments were independently selected by three outlets each — the Trump-Xi-Putin summit conclusion (WSJ, NYT, and reflected in NBC's Kyiv-strikes follow-on), the Senate permitting bill (NYT, NBC, AP), the Visa/Mastercard/JPMorgan/Chase data breach (WSJ, NYT, AP), and the Anthropic-IPO-triggered AI stock selloff (WSJ, NBC, AP) — but each outlet contributes exactly one distinct headline to each, reflecting genuine cross-outlet convergence on top-tier significance rather than within-outlet duplication.
