## Note

**Selection window:** Stories originally published between 2026-10-22 13:40 UTC and 2026-10-23 13:40 UTC. Items were filtered by original publication/pubDate (not update or retrieval time); substantially identical stories and canonical URLs were deduplicated before ranking.

**Access and feed limitations:**
- All eight WSJ feeds, ten NYT feeds, and eight NBC feeds fetched successfully with the specified browser-style user agent, yielding roughly 350 in-window candidate items combined.
- AP does not publish a general-purpose RSS feed. Per instructions, candidates were drawn from `news.google.com/rss/search?q=site:apnews.com+when:1d`, which returned 100 in-window items. Resolving Google News' redirect links directly (via `curl` or a raw fetch) was blocked — both approaches returned Google's automated-query/anti-bot interstitial rather than a 302 redirect, so the token-encoded destination could not be decoded with a simple HTTP request. `apnews.com` itself also refuses direct automated fetches. Canonical URLs were instead cross-verified using Bing News search (`site:apnews.com` plus story-specific keywords), matching each candidate's headline text and recency ("1 day ago" / "16h ago" / etc.) against the Google News candidate pool before accepting a URL. This successfully resolved and verified canonical `apnews.com` URLs for all five AP selections.
- One AP headline (item 3) is shown using the verified title under its canonical URL ("What to know about the AI-detected security bug..."), which differs slightly in wording from the Google News listing's duplicate headline for the same story ("Rush is on to patch a software vulnerability that could impact 70% of websites worldwide"); both describe the identical PHP-vulnerability development and only the verifiable title/URL pairing was used.

**Principal selection rationale:** Selection prioritized developments with durable, 30-day-plus consequences over virality: active war/conflict escalation (Iran-U.S./Strait of Hormuz tensions, the West Bank shooting and subsequent full lockdown), major tech/infrastructure risk (the PHP security flaw affecting a large share of the world's websites), court rulings with lasting electoral-law effect (the Supreme Court's New Hampshire student-ID decision), business/labor moves (American Express layoffs, Moderna's data-leak-driven selloff, a Russian oil tanker testing a new U.S.-Russia energy arrangement), governance/oversight matters (the DOJ inquiry into Corey Lewandowski), and public-health/science findings with lasting import (Ebola's widened spread, Europe's record heat deaths, the coral-reef collapse study). Heavily covered midterm-campaign-ad controversies (e.g., the Ohio governor's race ad fight) were deliberately excluded as partisan theater rather than durable developments, despite high volume in all four outlets' feeds.

**Strongest excluded candidate per outlet:**
- **WSJ:** The Pentagon's 10-year F-35 purchase commitment with Lockheed Martin — a substantial defense-procurement story, narrowly excluded in favor of five items judged more urgent or more directly consequential today.
- **NYT:** "Employers Shift Rising Health Care Costs Onto Millions of Workers" — a broad-reach economic story, excluded in favor of the West Bank escalation, which was judged to carry greater immediate international significance.
- **NBC:** "DHS watchdog refers its Corey Lewandowski investigation to Justice Department for review" — a solid governance story, excluded for space since WSJ's selections already cover the same underlying Lewandowski inquiry in this digest.
- **AP:** "Sharp drops for AI stocks overwhelm markets worldwide as oil prices jump" — a significant markets story, narrowly excluded in favor of the coral-reef study for a more durable, less day-to-day angle, and because NBC's selections already cover related market/AI-spending turbulence.

**Redundancy check:** No outlet's final five had three or more selections concerning the same underlying development. The West Bank shooting/lockdown and the PHP vulnerability each appear in two different outlets' top five (NYT/NBC and NYT/AP/NBC respectively), but each outlet's piece was independently the strongest available item from that outlet's own feeds, not inserted to pad a theme.
