## Note

Selection window: 2026-08-11T13:50 UTC through 2026-08-12T13:50 UTC, set from the timestamp of the freshest item returned across all feeds at collection time. Items were filtered on each item's own original publication timestamp, not on feed-retrieval or last-updated time.

All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds and all eight NBC section RSS feeds returned HTTP 200 and were fetched directly via the WebFetch tool. All ten `rss.nytimes.com` feed URLs failed when fetched through WebFetch ("unable to fetch from rss.nytimes.com"); they were retrieved instead by direct `curl` from this environment (all returned HTTP 200) and parsed as XML, with each item's own `pubDate` used for filtering exactly as with the other outlets. AP has no general-purpose RSS feed, so the Google News `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed was used for candidate discovery only. Its article links are opaque `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect tokens that resolve solely via client-side JavaScript, which a plain HTTP fetch or the WebFetch tool cannot execute (confirmed by testing both). Canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URLs were instead obtained by cross-referencing Google News headline text against AP's own topic hub pages (e.g. `apnews.com/hub/donald-trump`, `/hub/colombia`, `/hub/china`, `/hub/economy`, `/hub/middle-east`, `/hub/tariffs`), then confirming each candidate by fetching the live article page directly and reading its own `article:published_time` and `og:title` metadata — never a fabricated or guessed URL. Google News' own crawl-time `pubDate` was disregarded in favor of AP's page-level `published_time` per the publication-time filtering rule; one widely-circulated AP item on the Wisconsin primary ("Tuesday's takeaways: No clear direction for Wisconsin Democrats") carried an AP-stated original publish time of 2026-08-11T04:05 UTC — outside this window, with only a later in-place update falling inside it — so it was excluded from AP's list even though Google News' feed timestamped it Aug. 12.

Principal selection rationale: Trump's secret evacuation from Turkey aboard a decoy Air Force One, prompted by a reported Iranian threat, was independently selected as the top story by all four outlets — a rare, high-consequence disclosure about a sitting president's security that bears directly on the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict. Colombia's earthquake, with the death toll still climbing and thousands reported missing, was likewise independently selected by all four as a major humanitarian disaster. July's cooler-than-expected CPI inflation reading, a data point with direct bearing on Federal Reserve policy and household budgets, was independently selected by WSJ, NBC, and AP. David Crowley's narrow win over democratic-socialist Francesca Hong in Wisconsin's Democratic gubernatorial primary was independently selected by WSJ, NYT, and NBC as a bellwether for the party's centrist-progressive divide ahead of the midterms. The death of former Chinese Premier Zhu Rongji, architect of China's market reforms and WTO accession, was independently selected by NYT and AP as a story of lasting historical significance. Beyond these threads: Syria's death sentence for former dictator Bashar al-Assad, a rare instance of transitional justice reaching a deposed autocrat (WSJ); the Hormuz standoff's deepening drag on global oil supply, a distinct economic consequence of the Iran conflict from the Air Force One story (WSJ); David Ellison's Paramount-Warner Bros. deal wavering under state pressure, a live test of media-industry consolidation and ownership (NYT); the release of Marine veteran Robert Gilman after more than four years in Russian custody, a diplomacy story (NBC); and the government's court fight over refunding importers for tariffs found unlawful, a case with major fiscal and separation-of-powers stakes (AP). The European total solar eclipse, though widely covered, was treated as a scheduled/previewed astronomical event rather than a durable news development and was excluded on that basis.

Strongest excluded candidates: for WSJ, "U.S. Strikes on Yemen Last Year Killed and Wounded Hundreds, Pentagon Finds" — a serious accountability story, set aside to avoid over-weighting the Iran/Mideast conflict theater, which the selected Air Force One and Hormuz items already cover from distinct angles. For NYT, "Trump Administration Bars Medicaid Funding of Transition Treatment for Transgender Youth" — a durable federal healthcare-policy shift, edged out to keep the five spread across national security, disaster, electoral politics, international history, and media industry without overlap. For NBC, "3 dead, almost 1 million without power as severe storms strike Midwest" — a genuine infrastructure and public-safety story, passed over because the Colombia earthquake already filled the day's disaster slot. For AP, "ICE plans to give officers gloves that can deliver painful electric shocks" — a significant AP-exclusive policy and oversight story, narrowly excluded once national security, disaster, economics, international history, and trade law had filled AP's five slots.
