## Note

**Selection window:** Stories originally published between 2026-10-23 13:39 UTC and 2026-10-24 13:39 UTC. Items were filtered by original pubDate (not update or retrieval time); substantially identical stories and canonical URLs were deduplicated before ranking.

**Access and feed limitations:**
- All eight WSJ feeds, ten NYT feeds, and eight NBC feeds fetched successfully with the specified browser-style user agent, yielding roughly 320 in-window candidate items combined.
- One WSJ selection (item 3, the Alaska Senate lawsuit) arrived in its feed only as a promotional social-post sentence rather than a standalone headline; the published headline was reconstructed from the article's own URL slug, consistent with WSJ's house style and confirmed against wire coverage of the same filing.
- AP does not publish a general-purpose RSS feed. Per instructions, candidates were drawn from `news.google.com/rss/search?q=site:apnews.com+when:1d`, which returned 100 in-window items. Google's redirect links (`news.google.com/rss/articles/...`) could not be resolved to canonical `apnews.com` URLs: the current encoding no longer embeds a plaintext destination, and both direct requests and the non-RSS article page were intercepted by Google's automated-query interstitial. `apnews.com` itself also returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches. Headlines, bylines, and publication times for all five AP selections were instead cross-verified by locating the identical AP wire copy as republished verbatim (full text, AP byline, "Copyright The Associated Press" credit) on AP wire-service partner sites (ksl.com, cp24.com, abcnews.com, abc7news.com), confirming headline wording, author, and original timestamp matched the Google News candidate pool. Those partner-hosted mirror URLs are used in place of the unreachable apnews.com canonical links, and are disclosed here as a substitution rather than a true `apnews.com` URL.

**Principal selection rationale:** Selection prioritized developments with durable, 30-day-plus consequences over virality: the Justice Department's preemptive suit to block Alaska's Senate certification if the Republican incumbent loses (a novel, precedent-testing election-law action, covered independently by all four outlets); escalating, nationwide French civil unrest over living costs and school funding, met with tens of thousands of police; the Jordan church-funeral attack that killed seven and wounded dozens; the criminal charging of two former FBI officials over 2023 congressional testimony on the Hunter Biden laptop probe; Iran's reported "advanced stage" plotting against U.S. officials amid Middle East tensions; and durable business, science, and institutional stories (a melanoma mRNA vaccine's late-stage trial success, the IAEA's long-range nuclear-capacity forecast, the New York Archdiocese's $800M-plus abuse settlement, Kim Jong-un's first China visit in over a year, a Quest Diagnostics patient-data breach, and Middle East-driven oil-price pressure). Heavily covered day-to-day midterm-campaign-ad fights and celebrity health disclosures were deliberately excluded as partisan theater or private-life coverage rather than durable developments, despite high volume in all four outlets' feeds.

**Strongest excluded candidate per outlet:**
- **WSJ:** American Express's move to cut 3,500 jobs (5% of its global workforce) — a solid business/labor story, narrowly excluded in favor of five items judged more urgent internationally and legally.
- **NYT:** "Global Oil Price Hits $116 a Barrel, Highest Since April" — a strong markets story tied to Middle East risk, excluded for space since WSJ's own markets pick already covers that angle, in favor of broader institutional and geopolitical breadth (the Archdiocese settlement and Kim Jong-un's China visit).
- **NBC:** "The Dallas Cowboys cheerleaders are unionizing" — a genuine labor/culture story with industry-wide implications, excluded in favor of the Quest Diagnostics breach, judged to have broader and more durable consumer-protection consequences.
- **AP:** "Israel imposes closures across a West Bank city after attacks, and other Mideast news" — a major, ongoing Middle East conflict story, excluded because the Jordan church attack and the French unrest already provide this digest's AP selections with international-conflict and civil-unrest coverage, and a third such item would have crowded out law/governance, legal, and energy-policy developments.

**Redundancy check:** No outlet's final five had three or more selections concerning the same single underlying development. The Alaska Senate-certification lawsuit appears in all four outlets' top five (each outlet's own strongest available item on it, not inserted to pad a theme), and Middle East tension/conflict appears in two or three selections per outlet (WSJ: Jordan attack and oil prices; NYT: France and Iran threat warning; NBC: Iran threat warning) — but in each case these are distinct underlying developments (different countries, actors, or triggering events), not repeated coverage of one story.
