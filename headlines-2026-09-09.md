As instructed for domains that cannot be fetched directly, each AP headline was cross-verified using another reliable index: candidate stories were first identified from the specified Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`), then each finalist's exact headline, AP byline, and publication timestamp were independently confirmed on a verified AP wire-syndication partner page (a newspaper or broadcaster site carrying the unmodified AP wire text under an AP reporter's byline) that returns HTTP 200. All five AP links point to these verified syndication copies rather than apnews.com itself; all five carry a visible "Associated Press" byline and headline text identical to AP's own, and all five publication timestamps fall inside the selection window.

A second cross-outlet thread was the chaotic legal fight over Missouri's congressional map, where dueling Missouri Supreme Court and federal court rulings left the state's district lines unsettled eight weeks before the midterms, covered by all four outlets as a live constitutional/electoral story. A third thread was the U.S.-Canada trade war's sharp escalation, as the U.S. moved to ban Canadian dairy, alcohol, and motorcycle imports in response to Canada's new retaliatory tariffs, covered by WSJ, NBC, and AP. Beyond these threads, outlets diverged to capture other durable stories: a major transatlantic rift as European nations imposed trade bans on Israel over West Bank settlements, and OpenAI's contested claim to have solved a Millennium Prize math problem (WSJ); a report that Netanyahu was warned before the Oct. 7 attack and denies it, and a novel AI-built hacking tool capable of breaching phones without a click (NYT); China's pushback on U.S. accusations of systematic AI-model copying, and new detail on the fatal Miami cargo-plane crash (NBC); the human toll of a U.S. strike that killed dozens of children at an Iranian school, and an AP exclusive on new grand jury subpoenas in an investigation targeting the origins of the Trump-Russia probe (AP).
