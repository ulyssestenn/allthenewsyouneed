## Note

**Selection window:** 2026-11-07 13:39 UTC to 2026-11-08 13:39 UTC (original publication time, not update or feed-retrieval time).

**Access limitations:**
- `wsj.com` returns HTTP 401 to direct fetches, consistent with prior days, so WSJ headlines are reproduced verbatim from their RSS `<title>` fields. One of today's five (the Fed/central-bank item) came through only as a narrative-caption-style title, because WSJ's own feed did not carry a terser alternative for that article URL; it is reproduced exactly as published rather than rewritten, as on prior days.
- AP publishes no general-purpose RSS feed, so candidates were sourced via `news.google.com/rss/search?q=site:apnews.com+when:1d`, per the task's own instructions. `apnews.com` itself returns HTTP 403 to direct fetches. Canonical AP URLs were recovered by fetching each Google News redirect page's embedded `data-p` attribute (containing the signed article id, timestamp, and signature Google's own front end uses to resolve the link) and replaying Google News' internal `batchexecute` RPC call with that same payload — the identical request the Google News web app makes when a reader opens the link — which returns AP's canonical URL directly from Google's servers. AP's own article pages were never fetched directly for content, and no attempt was made to bypass AP's bot protection. Two of AP's five selections (the Nov. 7 and Nov. 8 Mideast items) resolve to AP's rolling daily "Mideast news" roundup pages rather than single-incident articles; Google News indexed each day's page under the day's lead bullet, which is reproduced here as the headline.
- All NYT and NBC feeds were fetched directly without issue.
- NBC's own feeds (world, politics, business, science, health, pop-culture) carried no in-window item on several major stories independently confirmed by the other three outlets today — the Houthi/Aramco strike on Riyadh, Eisenkot's bid to form Israel's next government, Iran's missile-range announcement, and the Schumer/Jeffries congressional-leadership stories. This is a genuine gap in NBC's syndicated RSS output for the window, not an editorial judgment that these stories are unimportant; it left NBC with only four items that cleared this digest's significance bar, rather than five. No fifth candidate was added to pad the list.
