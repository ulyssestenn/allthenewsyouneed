## Note

**Window:** Stories originally published between 2026-11-21 14:39 UTC and 2026-11-22 14:39 UTC.

**Access limitations:**
- Direct fetches of `apnews.com`, `wsj.com`, and `nytimes.com` article pages were blocked (Cloudflare challenge / bot protection), so verification relied on each outlet's RSS feed metadata (title, canonical link, `pubDate`) rather than the rendered page.
- AP has no general-purpose RSS feed. Candidates were sourced via a Google News search scoped to `site:apnews.com` with a 1-day window, as specified. Google News wraps AP links in redirect URLs; these were resolved to canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URLs using Google News' own signed-redirect decoding endpoint (not a general web search), which is authoritative for the mapping even though the destination pages themselves could not be fetched directly for additional confirmation.
- A general web-search tool used to cross-check a few AP and WSJ items returned mostly older, syndicated, or unrelated coverage — its index lagged behind stories only hours old. Feed metadata (not general search) was treated as the primary source of truth for timing and canonical URLs.

**Selection rationale:** Priority went to developments with durable international-security, policy, legal, and institutional consequences — the Mexico cartel-boss capture, North Korea's nuclear posture, the UAE's Hormuz oil workaround, COP31's collapse without a fossil-fuel agreement, Nvidia's SEC accounting probe and chip-smuggling case, a federal judge blocking a Trump homelessness policy, a White House order curbing contractors' dealings with Congress, and an apparent breach of an FBI criminal-justice database — over higher-volume but lower-durability content (sports scores, shopping deals, celebrity and fandom pieces, topic-hub index pages) that made up the bulk of the raw AP and WSJ candidate pools.

**Strongest excluded candidate per outlet:**
- **WSJ:** "A week of widely criticized choices has renewed calls within the administration to get rid of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin" — real governance significance, but more speculative internal-personnel politics than the harder international-security and trade stories selected.
- **NYT:** "Trump Moves to Restrict Congressional Oversight of Contractors" — the same underlying development as AP's selection on contractor oversight; excluded to avoid duplicating that story, with NYT's Trump-administration/judicial-clash slot already filled by the homelessness-policy ruling.
- **NBC:** "What Did the United States Gain From Trump's Trilateral Meeting?" — a companion analysis/video on the same Trump-Putin/Ukraine story already selected, more opinion-oriented and weaker than the direct report on the Kyiv-area strike.
- **AP:** "Voters oust some sheriffs who signed pacts to help Trump's immigration enforcement efforts" — a genuine durable governance/immigration-policy trend story, narrowly edged out by the scale and immediacy of the five selected items.

**Same-underlying-development check:** No outlet has three or more of its five selections concerning the same underlying development. Note that the Mexico cartel-boss capture appears once each in WSJ, NYT, and AP (independent editorial judgment across three outlets, not a single outlet's redundancy), and COP31's collapse appears once each in NYT, NBC, and AP for the same reason.
