## Note

Selection window: 2026-08-01T13:36 UTC through 2026-08-02T13:36 UTC (09:36 ET to 09:36 ET). All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool; items were filtered on `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time. wsj.com and nytimes.com both returned HTTP 401/403 (DataDome-protected) to direct fetch attempts from this environment, so WSJ and NYT selections rely on their own RSS `title`/`pubDate` fields as the verification source, consistent with prior digests. Two WSJ RSS items shared the same headline and slug with only a tracking query string (`?mod=...`) differing from another feed's copy of the identical article; these were treated as one candidate, not two.

AP does not publish a public RSS feed, so its 100-item candidate pool was built from Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed. Unlike in some prior sessions, apnews.com itself was directly fetchable in this environment, which allowed every AP candidate to be cross-verified against apnews.com's own site search and each article's live JSON-LD `datePublished` metadata rather than relying on Google News' feed-assigned pubDate alone. This caught three items Google News had surfaced with fresh-looking pubDates that were actually stale: "Spain installs sea barrier on Ceuta's border with Morocco after frontier rush that killed 67" (`datePublished` 2026-08-01T08:18:50Z, before the window, and the identical `apnews.com/article/migration-spain-ceuta-morocco-...` URL already used as AP's top story in yesterday's, 2026-08-01, digest); "UEFA declares no confidence in Infantino and vows accountability for failed World Cup equity plan" (`datePublished` 2026-08-01T10:17:46Z, before the window); and "Trump stands to profit off US policy announcements by selling fast access to his social media posts" (`datePublished` 2026-08-01T11:06:22Z, before the window, and the identical URL used as AP's #5 story yesterday). All three were dropped from today's AP pool. All five confirmed-final AP URLs carry `datePublished` timestamps between 2026-08-01T16:16:51Z and 2026-08-02T04:09:14Z.

Principal selection rationale: the dominant thread across all four outlets was Trump's Saturday-night decision to call off a planned new round of strikes on Iran after mediators reported the outlines of a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, ending (for now) an active five-month war — the single most consequential development of the window and this digest's top story everywhere it was covered.
