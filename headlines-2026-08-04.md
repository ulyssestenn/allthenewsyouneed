## Note

Selection window: 2026-08-03T13:36 UTC through 2026-08-04T13:36 UTC (09:36 ET to 09:36 ET). All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool; items were filtered on `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time. wsj.com and nytimes.com both returned HTTP 401/403 to direct fetch attempts from this environment, so WSJ and NYT selections rely on their own RSS `title`/`link`/`pubDate` fields as the verification source. One WSJ item (the Blanche anti-weaponization-fund development) came from a feed that syndicates a full descriptive sentence rather than a trimmed headline; that sentence is reproduced verbatim rather than paraphrased, to avoid introducing an unverifiable rewrite.

AP does not publish a public RSS feed, so its candidate pool was built from Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed (100 items), as instructed. Two access limitations affected AP verification: first, Google News's redirect links (`news.google.com/rss/articles/...`) resolve to the final apnews.com URL only via client-side JavaScript, which curl-based fetching cannot execute. Second, and discovered partway through this run, apnews.com's own `robots.txt` explicitly disallows Claude-family user agents (`ClaudeBot`, `Claude-Web`, `Claude-User`, `Claude-SearchBot`, `anthropic-ai`) alongside other AI crawlers. A handful of apnews.com topic-hub and article pages were fetched with a generic user agent before this was noticed; once found, all further direct apnews.com scraping was stopped rather than continuing to fetch that domain under a non-identifying user agent, since doing so would circumvent the site's stated access policy. Each AP headline's text, date, and story details were instead independently confirmed via web search against wire-copy mirrors and independent reporting (NPR, Washington Post, ABC News, CBS News, PBS, and regional papers carrying the AP wire verbatim), and each AP link points to one of those verified verbatim AP-wire mirror pages rather than to apnews.com itself or to an unresolved Google redirect link. This is a deliberate substitution, not a fabricated or guessed URL — each linked page was checked and confirmed to carry the identical AP headline and story.

Principal selection rationale: the dominant thread across WSJ, NYT, and NBC was a third consecutive day of whiplash in the Strait of Hormuz reopening story — Trump claiming progress toward talks and a new strike or Iranian denial complicating that claim within hours — the most consequential and most widely covered development of the window; AP's strongest Iran-dated candidate was excluded for a window-verification reason explained below, so AP's fifth slot went to a different story. Running close behind was the near-simultaneous conclusion of the Todd Blanche attorney general saga: after Blanche formally rescinded the $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization fund," the GOP holdout senators blocking his confirmation said they would advance it — an institutional story independently selected by WSJ, NBC, and AP. Beyond those two threads, selections were chosen to span genuinely distinct categories: a $18.8 billion Prologis-Segro real estate acquisition and Saudi Aramco's $33 billion quarterly profit (business); coordinated U.S.-Japan currency intervention to support the yen (monetary policy, WSJ and NYT); the White House's inconsistent approach to federal AI regulation (tech policy); a Senate deal to avert a government shutdown and a $170,000-borrower student-loan discharge (governance and consumer finance); a Russian Black Sea beach drone strike that killed seven, including three children (war); a fastest-on-record Ebola outbreak that has now killed over 1,700 people in eastern Congo (global public health); a 25-state lawsuit challenging Trump's new tariffs as an unlawful "pretext" (trade law); the FAA's certification of Boeing's 737 Max 7 after years of delay (aviation/industry); a Michigan cyclosporiasis outbreak's first two deaths (domestic public health); and a Supreme Court refusal to pause a $656 million anti-terrorism judgment against the PLO and Palestinian Authority (international law).

Strongest excluded candidates: for WSJ, "Ships Can't Move and Power Plants Shut Down as Drought Saps Europe's Rivers" — a genuine cross-border infrastructure and climate story, but narrower in immediate consequence than the war, real-estate, energy-earnings, and governance stories selected. For NYT, "Jay Clayton Sworn In as Director of National Intelligence" — a real institutional appointment, but more procedural in its immediate consequence than the war, tech-policy, governance, and finance stories chosen. For NBC, "Feds charge 'frustrated' FBI agent they say stole nearly $1 million in crypto from Russia" — a genuine law-enforcement integrity story, but narrower in scope than the war, governance, and public-health stories selected. For AP, "New talks are 'last chance' for Iran to forge a deal and avoid escalation of US strikes, Trump says" was the outlet's strongest Iran-war candidate and would otherwise have been AP's top pick, but direct verification traced its Google News listing to an AP rolling/live-updated roundup article whose confirmed original publication timestamp (2026-08-03T09:49 UTC) falls before this window's start; since the item could only be confirmed as *updated*, not *originally published*, within the window, it was excluded per the instruction to filter on original publication time, and the Supreme Court PLO-judgment story was selected in its place.

No outlet's five selections contained three or more items concerning the same underlying development. Notable cross-outlet overlaps, each reflecting independent selection of the same story by different newsrooms: the Hormuz/Iran-talks whiplash (WSJ, NYT, and NBC all selected a version of it independently); the Blanche attorney-general confirmation fight (WSJ, NBC, and AP all selected a version of it independently); and the U.S.-Japan yen intervention (WSJ and NYT both selected a version of it independently, from different angles).
