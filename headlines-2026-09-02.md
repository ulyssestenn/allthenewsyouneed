## Note

**Selection window:** 2026-09-01 13:39 UTC to 2026-09-02 13:39 UTC (24 hours), filtered by original publication timestamp (each outlet's RSS `<pubDate>`, or, for AP, each candidate's own `news:publication_date` from AP's public Google News-format sitemap), not update or retrieval time.

**Access notes:**
- All eight listed WSJ feeds, all ten listed NYT feeds, and all eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly and parsed successfully. No domain or feed was inaccessible.
- Both WSJ's and NYT's own article pages return anti-bot 401/403 responses to direct, unauthenticated requests (including with a standard browser user agent), independent of any specific URL — this is each outlet's standard bot/paywall gate, not a broken or fabricated link. All five WSJ selections and all five NYT selections are canonical article URLs taken directly from each outlet's own official RSS feeds (tracking query parameters stripped for the canonical form) and were not independently re-verified by HTTP fetch for this reason. All five NBC selections and all five AP selections were confirmed to resolve with HTTP 200.
- AP does not publish a general RSS feed. The Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`) was fetched for candidate discovery, but its links are opaque `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect tokens that do not resolve to `apnews.com` via direct HTTP request. AP's public Google News-format sitemap (`apnews.com/news-sitemap-content.xml`) was used instead as the primary candidate pool, each item carrying its own `news:publication_date` and canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL; the sitemap also carries many Spanish-language AP en Español items and a large volume of routine sports-transaction/box-score/game-preview items and service content (quizzes, top-10 lists, photo galleries), which were excluded from consideration in favor of standard dedicated news articles.

**Selection rationale:** The dominant story of the cycle was the continuing, escalating U.S.-Iran conflict: overnight U.S. strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, Iranian retaliatory strikes on Gulf allies and (reportedly) U.S. interests, and the resulting spike in oil prices and global bond yields. Each outlet was allowed to select the distinct facet of that story most central to its own coverage — WSJ the strikes-for-Hormuz-control framing, NYT the morning-after damage assessment, NBC the targeting of American bases and the oil-price/inflation angle, and AP the attack on Gulf allies and Bahrain's interception — rather than collapsing it into a single redundant headline. Beyond the war, durable governance and market stories dominated: a global bond sell-off and G20 tensions over China's "overcapacity" exports threatening borrowing costs worldwide (WSJ, NYT); continuing fallout from Army Secretary Dan Driscoll's resignation as Defense Secretary Hegseth moves against more officers (NYT); a fresh whistleblower warning about the Postal Service's mail-ballot system ahead of the midterms, paired with a Texas county's move to cut polling sites — both concrete election-administration stories (NYT, NBC); California's sweeping new AI and social-media regulations (NBC); OpenAI's decision to restrict its Astra model after rating it a "critical" cyber risk, a first-of-its-kind AI-safety intervention (WSJ); a UN finding that the world will miss its 1.5-degree climate target (NYT); a UN nuclear watchdog's finding that Syria had secretly built a reactor under Assad (AP); a court striking down New York's law charging fossil-fuel producers for climate damages (AP); Congo's Ebola outbreak surpassing 3,000 deaths (AP); Texas hospitals facing $27 million a day in lost Medicaid funding (AP); Chevron's $7 billion Venezuela expansion (WSJ, NYT); and the Charlie Kirk assassination case being sent to trial with the death penalty in play (WSJ).

**Strongest excluded candidates:**
- **WSJ:** "Germany Blames Russia for Explosive Drones Sent to Leipzig Airport" — a genuine hybrid-war escalation story, narrowly excluded because it is largely an attribution follow-up to an incident from weeks earlier, and WSJ's slate already carried two international-conflict/markets stories.
- **NYT:** "Judge Lets Case Against Tyler Robinson, Charlie Kirk Shooting Suspect, Go to Trial" — a real and durable legal story, excluded in favor of NYT's institutional and global slate, and already represented in WSJ's five.
- **NBC:** "Student who felt 'humiliated' by ICE ankle monitor dies by suicide" — carries a genuine institutional-policy angle (immigration enforcement mandates), but was excluded in favor of concrete, already-enacted governance developments (funding bill, polling-site cuts, AI regulation) rather than a story whose durable significance rests partly on individual tragedy.
- **AP:** "MLB's Los Angeles Angels to be sold to Stan Kroenke" — a rare, durable sports-ownership change, narrowly excluded in favor of stories with broader institutional and public-health consequence (the Ebola death toll, the Syria nuclear reactor revelation, the Medicaid funding crisis, and the climate-liability court ruling).

**Redundancy check:** No single outlet had three or more of its own five selections tied to the same underlying development. Across outlets, the U.S.-Iran war was independently selected by all four outlets, each covering a distinct facet (Hormuz strikes, overnight damage assessment, attacks on U.S. bases and the oil-price effect, and the Gulf-ally/Bahrain angle); Chevron's Venezuela expansion was independently selected by WSJ and NYT; and the bond-market/G20 story was independently selected by WSJ and NYT. Each was retained under its own outlet's five because it represents that outlet's genuinely highest-ranked, non-duplicative story, and cross-outlet convergence on the day's most significant developments is expected rather than penalized.
