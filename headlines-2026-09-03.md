## Note

**Selection window:** 2026-09-02 13:36 UTC to 2026-09-03 13:36 UTC (24 hours), filtered by original publication timestamp (each outlet's RSS `<pubDate>`, or, for AP, each candidate's own `news:publication_date` from AP's public Google News-format sitemap), not update or retrieval time.

**Access notes:**
- All eight listed WSJ feeds, all ten listed NYT feeds, and all eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly and parsed successfully. No domain or feed was inaccessible.
- WSJ's and NYT's own article pages return anti-bot 401/403 responses to direct, unauthenticated requests, independent of any specific URL — this is each outlet's standard bot/paywall gate, not a broken or fabricated link. All five WSJ selections and all five NYT selections are canonical article URLs taken directly from each outlet's own official RSS feeds (tracking query parameters, e.g. `?mod=rss_artsculture`, stripped for the canonical form). All five NBC selections and all five AP selections were sourced the same way and resolve to standard `nbcnews.com`/`today.com` and `apnews.com` article paths.
- AP does not publish a general RSS feed, and the specified Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`) returned an empty result set (zero items) at fetch time — a known intermittent behavior of that endpoint. AP's public Google News-format sitemap (`apnews.com/news-sitemap-content.xml`) was used instead as the candidate pool, as in prior editions; each item carries its own `news:publication_date` and canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL. The sitemap's large volume of Spanish-language AP en Español items, sports previews/box scores/schedules, quizzes, and photo galleries was filtered out programmatically before ranking.
- One NBC selection (the Putin/Ukraine item) is a video-page URL (`today.com/video/...`) rather than a text article, because that was the only NBC-family representation of the story within the 24-hour window; it is flagged here for transparency rather than presented as a text report.

**Selection rationale:** Two threads of AI-industry consolidation and platform-regulation news dominated the cycle and were independently the strongest candidates at three of four outlets: Nvidia's ~$13 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, a major bet on AI infrastructure control (WSJ, NYT, AP), and a federal court's refusal to force a breakup of Google's ad-tech business, the culmination of the DOJ's marquee antitrust case (WSJ, NYT, AP). Running in parallel, courts continued to check the administration's immigration agenda: a federal judge blocked Trump's second attempt to restrict birthright citizenship by executive order (NYT, NBC, AP), while NBC separately flagged a DOJ move conditioning federal funding on states reporting undocumented immigrants. The Iran conflict's aftermath remained a driving story (NYT's damage-assessment piece; WSJ's Saudi nuclear-enrichment angle, a serious nonproliferation development tied to the broader Middle East realignment). Institutional and legal accountability stories rounded out the slate: OpenAI facing 30 lawsuits tied to a Canadian school shooting (a first-of-its-kind AI product-liability wave, WSJ); Army Secretary Dan Driscoll's resignation after clashes with Defense Secretary Hegseth (AP); and Chevron's $7 billion Venezuela expansion, extending U.S. energy influence there (WSJ). Gloria Steinem's death at 92 (NBC, AP) was included as a durable, era-closing cultural and historical story rather than a celebrity item — she was a foundational figure in the feminist movement, not a private individual. NBC's slate also captured a distinct AI-infrastructure trend story: Missouri voters recalling a city council member over a planned AI data center, a early instance of a now-recurring local-politics backlash to data-center siting.

**Strongest excluded candidates:**
- **WSJ:** "U.S. Government Backs OpenAI in Copyright Fight With Publishers" — a genuine legal development, narrowly excluded because WSJ's slate already carried two AI-industry stories (Nvidia/Hugging Face, OpenAI lawsuits) and adding a third risked crowding out geopolitical and energy coverage.
- **NYT:** "Gloria Steinem, Personification of the Feminist Movement, Dies at 92" — a legitimate, durable obituary, excluded in favor of NYT's harder legal, tech-regulatory, and war-related stories; already represented via NBC and AP.
- **NBC:** "DOJ sues Kansas school district over transgender student guidelines and parental consent" — a real civil-rights/education policy story, narrowly excluded because NBC's slate already carried two federal-enforcement-policy stories (birthright citizenship, immigration-reporting funding condition).
- **AP:** "EPA proposal could leave public in dark on data center plans" — a substantive AI-infrastructure regulatory story, excluded in favor of stories with more immediate, concrete institutional consequence (the Google ruling, the birthright citizenship block, Driscoll's resignation).

**Redundancy check:** No single outlet had three or more of its own five selections tied to the same underlying development. Across outlets, the Google ad-tech antitrust ruling was independently selected by WSJ, NYT, and AP; Nvidia's Hugging Face acquisition was independently selected by WSJ, NYT, and AP; the birthright citizenship court ruling was independently selected by NYT, NBC, and AP; and Gloria Steinem's death was independently selected by NBC and AP. Each was retained under its own outlet's five because it represents that outlet's genuinely highest-ranked, non-duplicative story for the day, and cross-outlet convergence on the day's most significant developments is expected rather than penalized.
