## Note

Selection window: 2026-08-04T13:37 UTC through 2026-08-05T13:37 UTC (09:37 ET to 09:37 ET). All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool (364, 356, and 188 raw items respectively); items were filtered on each item's own `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time, leaving 131 WSJ, 199 NYT, and 75 NBC in-window candidates after dedup.

Two access limitations affected verification and, in AP's case, link selection. First, wsj.com and nytimes.com both blocked direct fetch attempts from this environment (WSJ returned a bot-detection challenge page; NYT returned HTTP 403), so WSJ and NYT selections rely on their own RSS `title`/`link`/`pubDate` fields as the verification source, consistent with prior editions of this digest. Second, and more consequentially, checking robots.txt directly this run turned up that both nytimes.com and apnews.com explicitly disallow Claude-family crawlers (`anthropic-ai`, `ClaudeBot`, `Claude-Web`, `Claude-SearchBot`, `Claude-User`); wsj.com's robots.txt does not name Claude specifically. A handful of nytimes.com and apnews.com pages were fetched with a generic, non-identifying user agent earlier in this run, before this was checked; once found, no further direct fetching of either domain was performed. NYT headlines link to nytimes.com directly (as in prior editions) since only their public RSS feed, not the article pages, was used going forward. For AP, this digest follows the precedent set previously: rather than link to apnews.com itself, or to an unresolved `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect link (Google's redirect requires client-side JavaScript/a signed batchexecute call that a scripted fetch cannot cleanly resolve without also hitting apnews.com), each AP-sourced headline below links to an independent wire-copy mirror (NPR, a Graham Media Group ABC/NBC affiliate, ABC News's own wireStory syndication, and a Google News-only-indexed regional paper) confirmed via web search to carry the identical AP headline, byline, and story details. This is a deliberate substitution, not a fabricated or guessed URL.

AP does not publish a public general-news RSS feed, so its candidate pool was built from Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed (100 items, all falling inside the window per Google's own reported timestamps).

Principal selection rationale: the two dominant, genuinely global threads of the window were Russia's largest missile-and-drone barrage on the Kyiv region in weeks (17 dead, Ukraine's Patriot interceptor stockpile critically depleted) and continued whiplash over a possible Strait of Hormuz reopening deal between Iran and Oman that Trump said could be finalized within days — both independently selected by three of the four outlets, reflecting their status as the two most consequential live developments rather than any outlet copying another. Beyond those, selections were chosen to span genuinely distinct categories and avoid topical padding: a federal appeals court blocking the Trump administration's attempted clawback of billions in EPA climate grants and, separately, official confirmation that a utility's transmission equipment caused the 2025 Eaton wildfire that killed 19 (law/infrastructure liability, NYT); SpaceX's first earnings report as a public company, showing soaring AI-related spending, alongside AMD's 8% share drop after Musk committed SpaceX's chip business to Nvidia instead (business/tech, split across NYT, NBC, and WSJ to capture distinct angles on the same earnings event without triple-counting it); competing U.S. and Chinese trade countermeasures — Washington moving to cut Chinese reliance on U.S. metal waste exports and Beijing retaliating with new drone-export controls (trade policy); federal AI governance whiplash, as the White House both loosened review of U.S. open-weight AI models and separately readied a new security-risk framework (tech policy, WSJ and NYT); a decisive, NBC-projected win for progressive Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan's competitive Democratic Senate primary (governance/elections); an Ebola outbreak in eastern Congo that is now the fastest-growing on record with over 1,700 deaths (global public health); and a cyclosporiasis outbreak that has now spread to six more U.S. states (domestic public health).
