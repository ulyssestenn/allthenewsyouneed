## Note

**Selection window:** 2026-08-15 13:37 UTC to 2026-08-16 13:37 UTC (24 hours), filtered by original publication timestamp (each outlet's RSS `<pubDate>`, or `article:published_time` page metadata for AP), not update or retrieval time.

**Access notes:**
- All listed WSJ and NYT feeds were fetched directly and parsed successfully. All eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly, but most (business, health, science, pop-culture, and much of world/us) contained no items published within the 24-hour window — NBC's non-flagship feeds update infrequently — leaving a thin, mostly video-clip-driven candidate pool concentrated in the news/politics/us feeds.
- WSJ's `socialpoliticsfeed`, `socialeconomyfeed`, and `socialhealth` feeds supply social-media teaser text (sentence case, first-person "writes WSJ's..." framing) in the `<title>` field rather than the actual headline; WSJ's `RSSWorldNews`, `WSJcomUSBusiness`, `RSSWSJD`, `RSSUSnews`, and `RSSArtsCulture` feeds use true Title Case headlines. To avoid publishing a fabricated or guessed headline, today's WSJ selections were drawn only from the headline-safe feeds; a strong candidate from `socialeconomyfeed` (see below) was excluded solely because its real headline could not be verified.
- WSJ and NYT article pages return 401/403 to non-browser HTTP requests even though their RSS feeds are open, so WSJ/NYT links above are used verbatim from each outlet's own RSS `<link>` field (WSJ links with the `?mod=...` tracking parameter stripped), consistent with each feed's own headline text.
- AP does not publish a general RSS feed. Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`) was used for initial discovery, but its `<pubDate>` reflects Google's crawl/discovery time, not AP's original publication time — two Google-surfaced candidates ("Democrats' 2028 presidential calendar...", published 2026-08-15T04:01 UTC per AP's own `article:published_time`, and "Brazil's Lula triggers reciprocity process...", published 2026-08-14T17:52 UTC) turned out to predate the selection window once checked against AP's page metadata, despite Google listing them as same-day. Because of this unreliability, final AP candidate discovery instead used apnews.com's own homepage and topic-hub/search pages (directly fetchable, unlike individual Google News redirect links, which resolve via opaque JS tokens), with every candidate's `article:published_time` meta tag checked directly against the window before inclusion.

**Selection rationale:** Prioritized developments with durable, 30-day-relevant consequences: an intensifying Iran conflict now spanning captured-pilot claims, Qatar diplomacy, a Chinese refinery bankrolling Tehran, and a shift toward economic-pressure strategy; a NATO member downing a Russian drone over Romania and Ukraine's largest aerial attack of the war on Russian soil, both raising direct-escalation stakes; the Pentagon's internal fight over AI dominance against China; a hate-crime prosecution tied to the Manhattan synagogue attack; a legal-ethics question over a firm's tie to the judge in Trump's hush-money trial; Berkshire Hathaway's disclosed portfolio shifts into Alphabet and homebuilders; a cooling July inflation print; the Democratic Party formally setting its 2028 primary calendar; a federal vaccine-policy change; and a rescue operation still underway after Indonesia's magnitude-7.7 earthquake death toll climbed to 53. Deprioritized: WSJ/NYT/NBC lifestyle, food, arts-calendar, and shopping content; routine political interview segments; sports recaps and transactions; and stories whose underlying event (the Idaho abortion ruling, the FBI homicide-rate report, the White House ballroom Supreme Court appeal, the Maryland digital-ad-tax ruling, and the Luigi Mangione plea) had already been featured in a prior day's digest.
