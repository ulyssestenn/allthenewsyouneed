## Note

**Selection window:** Original publication between 2026-11-24 14:39 UTC and 2026-11-25 14:39 UTC.

**Access and verification:** All WSJ (Dow Jones), NYT, and NBC RSS feeds listed in the sourcing instructions were reachable directly. AP has no general RSS feed; AP candidates were sourced via the Google News `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed. Each selected story's canonical `apnews.com` URL was resolved from Google's redirect wrapper using a headless-browser fetch (Google's redirect requires JS execution; a direct HTTP fetch of the wrapped link returns a 302 back to itself rather than the publisher URL). A subsequent direct fetch of the resolved `apnews.com` pages to confirm exact AP headline wording was blocked by a Cloudflare interstitial, so AP headline text is rendered from the Google News feed's listing title (with the trailing "- AP News" suffix removed) rather than a scrape of the live page; the underlying facts and canonical URLs were independently cross-verified against the corresponding WSJ/NYT/NBC coverage of the same stories.

**Principal selection rationale:** The dominant throughline of the cycle was a fresh round of trade escalation: the Trump administration unveiled new tariffs on China, the EU, Brazil, and other countries over subsidized industrial production, a concrete policy action (not a preview) that all four outlets led or near-led with. Beyond that, selections favor resolved, concrete developments with durable institutional consequences — a federal jury's guilty verdict against Huawei, a breach of U.S. intelligence-agency computer systems, an elite Chinese Communist Party corruption investigation, a historic U.S. House majority confirmed in the midterms, a hot inflation reading locking in a near-certain Fed rate move, a closed 20-month deportation legal saga, a third measles death as an outbreak spreads to the general public, and allegations of rape and forced labor at an ICE detention facility — over previews, proposals, market gyrations, or the softer, already-well-covered angles of the ongoing Jensen Huang/Nvidia saga (his apology, his possible pardon, market reaction to his return), which were deliberately underweighted here since the reinstatement itself was already the lead story in yesterday's digest.

**Strongest excluded candidates:**
- **WSJ:** "EU Lays out Plan to Boost Quantum Technology," detailing a $55 billion Sovereign Tech Equity Fund — a significant industrial/tech-policy story, but a funding proposal with less immediate consequence than the selected five.
- **NYT:** "Trump Administration Pauses Joint Operations With Ecuador" after an Ecuadorean strike killed an accused gang leader — a substantive foreign/military-policy story, but narrower in scope (a single bilateral relationship) than the selected items.
- **NBC:** "Judge orders Georgia's Fulton County to turn over copies of 2020 ballots to Trump-allied election board" — a real election-law story, but a procedural step in an ongoing fight rather than a resolved development.
- **AP:** "Solomon Islands signs treaty with Australia that checks China's influence in the South Pacific" — a durable Pacific-geopolitics story, narrowly excluded in favor of keeping AP's China coverage to one distinct angle (the Shanghai party-chief purge) and prioritizing the CIA/intelligence breach for its more direct domestic consequence.

**Redundancy check:** No outlet's final five contains three or more selections concerning the same underlying development. Each outlet carries at most one Jensen Huang/Nvidia-related item (WSJ: his coming Capitol Hill testimony; NYT: the Justice Department's probe into diverted Nvidia chips; NBC and AP: none), so no outlet risked over-weighting that single story. The new tariff announcement appears as the top story across all four outlets, but this reflects independent, shared significance — a concrete, same-day policy action — rather than redundant selection within any one outlet.
