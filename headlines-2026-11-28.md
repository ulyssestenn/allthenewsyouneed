## Note

**Selection window:** Original publication between 2026-11-27 14:40 UTC and 2026-11-28 14:40 UTC.

**Access and verification:** All WSJ (Dow Jones), NYT, and NBC RSS feeds listed in the sourcing instructions were reachable directly (NBC's feeds required following an HTTP redirect, handled with `curl -L`). AP has no general RSS feed; AP candidates were sourced via the Google News `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed. Each selected story's canonical `apnews.com` URL was resolved from Google's redirect wrapper using a headless-browser fetch (a direct HTTP fetch of the wrapped link returns a 302 back to itself rather than the publisher URL, since the redirect requires JS execution). AP headline text is rendered from the Google News feed's listing title (with the trailing "- AP News" suffix removed) rather than a scrape of the live apnews.com page, consistent with prior digests, since direct fetches of apnews.com pages are liable to be blocked by a Cloudflare interstitial. Many Google News AP results were topic/tag pages, election-results trackers, or sports roundups rather than news articles; these were discarded during candidate collection, not treated as stories.

**Principal selection rationale:** Three threads dominated the 24-hour cycle and were covered from distinct angles across outlets without redundancy within any single outlet's five. First, an escalation around Iran: the U.S. Navy struck a merchant vessel attempting to run its blockade of Iranian ports (WSJ, AP), a concrete military action with durable regional consequences. Second, the fallout from U.S. AI and China policy: WSJ's look at the domestic political cost of Trump's embrace of Jensen Huang, NYT's companion piece on Trump's embrace of Xi and Huang stunning China critics in Washington, and AP's report that a federal jury found Huawei guilty of a trade-secrets conspiracy — three genuinely distinct consequences (domestic political risk, foreign-policy realignment, and a criminal conviction) rather than one story repeated. Third, the war in Ukraine and the wider Gaza conflict continued to generate durable developments: Ukraine's long-range strikes growing in scale (NYT), Starlink's battlefield constraints on Ukraine (WSJ), the EU's stalled $49 billion Ukraine loan (AP), an Israeli strike that killed two Gaza clinic workers (NYT, AP), and Zelenskyy's rebuke of Trump's description of Putin as a "friend" (NBC). Elsewhere, two major court developments stood out: the U.S. Supreme Court agreeing to decide whether assault-weapons bans violate the Second Amendment (AP) and Canada's top court upholding Quebec's religious-symbols ban (NYT). On U.S. domestic policy and security: millions of children losing food stamps under SNAP cuts (NYT) and a hack penetrating an Air Force computing platform (NBC). NBC's pool was thin on durable news in this cycle; its final two selections (a CDC-confirmed cause for a norovirus outbreak that sickened 268 cruise passengers, and an ICE arrest of a lottery winner touching on immigration-enforcement practice) were included only after weaker crime-of-the-day and celebrity-health items were excluded. Deprioritized throughout: Black Friday shopping coverage, the Macy's parade delay, Princess Kate's documentary, NFL/NBA results, and the Thanksgiving-storm recovery stories beyond NBC's single infrastructure pick (to avoid padding multiple outlets with the same weather follow-up).
