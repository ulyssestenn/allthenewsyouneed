## Note

**Selection window:** Original publication between 2026-11-26 14:40 UTC and 2026-11-27 14:40 UTC.

**Access and verification:** All WSJ (Dow Jones), NYT, and NBC RSS feeds listed in the sourcing instructions were reachable directly. AP has no general RSS feed; AP candidates were sourced via the Google News `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed. Each selected story's canonical `apnews.com` URL was resolved from Google's redirect wrapper using a headless-browser fetch (a direct HTTP fetch of the wrapped link returns a 302 back to itself rather than the publisher URL, since the redirect requires JS execution). A subsequent direct fetch of the resolved `apnews.com` pages to confirm exact headline wording was blocked by a Cloudflare "Just a moment..." interstitial, so AP headline text is rendered from the Google News feed's listing title (with the trailing "- AP News" suffix removed) rather than a scrape of the live page. Many Google News AP results were topic/tag pages ("Inflation - AP News," "Minnesota - AP News," etc.) rather than articles; these were discarded during candidate collection, not treated as stories.

**Principal selection rationale:** No single story dominated the cycle the way yesterday's FCC/ABC ruling did; selections instead favor concrete, resolved developments with durable institutional consequences spread across conflict, governance, and economics. On the international side: an Iranian drone strike killing two Italians in Kuwait (WSJ, NYT), Zelenskyy's warning of an imminent Russian strike on Ukraine's power grid paired with EU leaders splitting over a $49 billion Ukraine loan (AP), and protests in Irbil as Baghdad moves on Kurdish oil and border control (AP). On institutions and economics: the selection of Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next ECB president (WSJ, NYT), France's economy tipping into recession (WSJ), Argentina's central bank losing the ability to print money for the government (AP), and Trump calling pre-election GDP figures "fake" and demanding a Commerce Department investigation (AP) — a direct challenge to the independence of U.S. economic statistics. On domestic policy and safety: new evidence the Army knew earlier than disclosed about the explosive risk behind the Iowa lead detonation (NYT), SNAP cuts threatening free school meals (NYT), Pennsylvania's measles outbreak — the nation's worst (WSJ), and a FedEx jet overrunning the runway at Miami International (NBC). Nvidia's halt of chip shipments to Southeast Asia continued to develop and is carried via NBC's same-day report; it was deliberately not re-selected for WSJ or NYT, since the NYT already ran this as a top pick in yesterday's digest and a same-day WSJ follow-up would have duplicated ground covered within the last 24 hours rather than reflecting new publication. Deprioritized throughout: Black Friday shopping coverage (lifestyle/service filler), the Aaron Rodgers–Steelers saga and other NFL results (routine sports), and the pregnant-inmate-escape story (crime-of-day).

**Strongest excluded candidates:**
- **WSJ:** "Nvidia Tells Partners to Pause Shipments to Southeast Asia" — a legitimate same-day WSJ report, but excluded because it covers the identical underlying development the NYT already featured as a top pick in yesterday's (2026-11-26) digest; selecting it today would have re-covered rather than advanced that story within WSJ's own five.

- **NBC:** "Pregnant inmate escapes from New Mexico corrections facility" — a real news event, but a crime-of-the-day story without durable institutional consequence, deprioritized per the selection standard.
- **AP:** "French cinema star Gérard Depardieu ordered to stand trial for rape and sexual assault" — a significant legal/cultural story in its own right, narrowly excluded in favor of five picks with clearer immediate institutional and economic consequences.

**Redundancy check:** No outlet's final five contains three or more selections concerning the same underlying development. The Iranian drone attack on Kuwait appears in two outlets (WSJ, NYT), and the Russia-Ukraine war appears via two distinct AP angles (the power-grid attack warning and the EU budget/loan split) — each reflecting independent, genuinely distinct consequences rather than redundant selection within any single outlet's list.
