**Access notes:** All eight listed WSJ feeds, all ten listed NYT feeds, and all eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly with the specified user agent and parsed successfully as RSS/XML; no feed was inaccessible. This yielded 355 raw WSJ items (29 in-window after dedup), 281 raw NYT items (69 in-window), and 150 raw NBC items (16 in-window). **AP resolution:** `apnews.com` itself returns HTTP 403 to both direct `curl` and WebFetch (a Cloudflare "Just a moment…" bot challenge on every path, including the homepage), so, per the source instructions, AP candidates were pulled from the specified Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`), yielding 100 candidates in the window. A large share of these were AP topic/hub pages (e.g., "Virginia - apnews.com," "Artificial intelligence - apnews.com"), author-archive pages, or routine sports recaps rather than distinct news articles, and were excluded. Each Google News redirect link for a genuine article was resolved to its canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL via Google News's own internal article-redirect endpoint (`news.google.com/_/DotsSplashUi/data/batchexecute`, method `Fbv4je`) — the same mechanism the Google News web app uses when a reader clicks a headline — rather than a plain HTTP redirect, which Google News does not serve to non-browser clients. Each resolved URL was then cross-checked against at least one AP-wire mirror site (WRAL.com and/or News4Jax.com, which republish AP copy verbatim) confirming an identical headline, byline, and matching URL-hash fragment, satisfying the "reliable index" cross-verification called for when a domain can't be fetched directly. One AP candidate returned by the search ("MS NOW, CNN and Politico say their journalists were denied access to White House after Trump ban") resolved to the exact same `apnews.com` URL already used in yesterday's (2026-09-19) digest — it was excluded as a same-article Google News re-crawl, not a new development, and replaced with the next-strongest genuinely new candidate.
