**Access notes:**
- All eight listed WSJ feeds, all ten listed NYT feeds, and all eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly and parsed successfully (891 raw items across WSJ/NYT/NBC; 290 fell inside the 24-hour window before deduplication). No domain or feed was inaccessible.
- Several WSJ social feeds (`socialpoliticsfeed`, `socialeconomyfeed`) publish a full teaser sentence in the RSS `<title>` field rather than the article's actual headline. Where this occurred, the canonical headline was derived from the article's own URL slug (consistent with prior days' methodology) rather than quoting the teaser sentence verbatim; affected picks are WSJ #3, #4, and #5.
- One WSJ item ("U.S. to Take Control of Much of Venezuela's Proven Oil Reserves," `.../trump-administration-in-advanced-talks-for-stake-in-venezuelan-oil-fields-819546d3`) reused the identical canonical URL already selected in yesterday's (2026-08-28) digest as WSJ's #4 pick; WSJ appears to have updated that existing article's headline rather than publishing a new one. Per the original-publication-time rule, it was excluded, and a separate, genuinely new WSJ URL on the same Venezuela oil story ("Chevron, Other U.S. Firms Near Deal to Invest Billions in Venezuelan Oil Fields," published 2026-08-28 14:08 UTC) was used in its place.
- AP does not publish a general RSS feed. The Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`) was fetched for candidate discovery, but its links are opaque `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect tokens that do not resolve to `apnews.com` via direct HTTP request (`curl -L` returns the same token URL; the redirect is JavaScript-driven). AP's own site (`apnews.com`) was directly reachable with a browser-style user agent, and AP's public Google News-format sitemap (`apnews.com/news-sitemap-content.xml`, permitted for general user agents under AP's `robots.txt`) was used instead to independently discover and verify AP's own candidate pool, each with its own `publication_date` and canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL, cross-checked against each article's own `datePublished` JSON-LD page metadata.
- Two strong AP candidates surfaced by Google News were excluded once their canonical page's own original publish time proved to fall just outside the window: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks (AP `publication_date` 2026-08-28 13:22 UTC, 14 minutes before this window opened — NBC's own in-window article on the same speech was used instead, so the story still appears via NBC) and the Longview, Washington chemical-tank investigative finding (AP `publication_date` 2026-08-28 12:13 UTC, about 83 minutes before this window opened — NYT's own in-window article on the same investigative finding was considered but not selected for NYT's five).
- WSJ and NYT article pages return 401/403 to non-browser HTTP requests even though their RSS feeds are open, so WSJ/NYT links above are used verbatim from each outlet's own RSS `<link>` field (tracking query parameters, e.g. `?mod=rss_worldnews`, stripped to canonical form), consistent with each feed's own headline text.
