## Note

Selection window: 2026-07-27T13:40 UTC through 2026-07-28T13:40 UTC. All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, and all eight NBC section RSS feeds listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 and were used to build each outlet's candidate pool; items were filtered on `pubDate`, not on feed-retrieval time. WSJ and NYT article pages return bot-protection placeholders (HTTP 401 / 403) to automated requests without a browser session, so their links could not be live-verified by fetching the article page directly; they are the exact `<link>` values published in each outlet's own RSS feed, with WSJ's tracking query parameters (e.g., `?mod=rss_Technology`, `?mod=pls_whats_news_us_business_f`) stripped for a clean canonical URL. The WSJ mail-ballots headline was reconstructed from its URL slug because that item's only `<title>` in the WSJ politics feed was a sentence-style social-media caption rather than a standalone headline.

AP does not publish a public RSS feed. Its candidate pool (99 items) was built from Google News's `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed (HTTP 200). Rather than leaving `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect links in place, each selected item's signed redirect parameters (`data-n-a-sg`, `data-n-a-ts`, `data-n-a-id`) were extracted from the Google News interstitial page and submitted to Google News's own `batchexecute` decode endpoint to resolve the canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL; all five resolved URLs were then confirmed live with HTTP 200 direct requests. NBC's own RSS feeds were heavily weighted toward shopping guides, product roundups, and short video clips, which were excluded as lifestyle/service filler under the selection standard; one NBC selection (the DOJ Biden-audio story) links to a `today.com` video page because that was the only article NBC's own politics feed carried on that development — Today is NBCUniversal News Group's syndication partner and the item appeared directly in the assigned `feeds.nbcnews.com/nbcnews/public/politics` feed.

The dominant story of the cycle was a magnitude-7.1 earthquake in southern Japan (Kumamoto), which triggered tsunami warnings, an apparent mall-structure collapse trapping shoppers, and damage to historic Kumamoto Castle; all four outlets covered it independently and it is the lead story for WSJ, NBC, and AP. A second widely covered story was the Trump administration's request that the Supreme Court allow a federal order restricting mail-in ballots and creating voter-eligibility lists ahead of the midterms — a significant election-law development that all four outlets selected independently. A third thread concerned durable geopolitical/military escalation risk: Ukraine's strike on an Iranian vessel supplying Russia across the Caspian Sea (WSJ, NYT) and rising Trump–Netanyahu-era tensions tied to the Iran war (NBC). A fourth thread was large-scale AI/technology infrastructure investment, led by OpenAI's reported $500 billion Nvidia-backed data-center deal (NYT) and Visa's 2,600-job cut tied to payments-industry disruption (WSJ). Rounding out selections were the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo surpassing 3,200 cases (AP), Johnson & Johnson's $5.5 billion talc-litigation settlement (WSJ, AP), a federal judge blocking Minnesota's prediction-market ban (NBC), escalating Pakistan–Afghanistan border violence with nearly 500 Afghan civilian deaths since October (AP), contested and violent voting in Pakistani-administered Kashmir (NYT), and the Justice Department's release of Biden-era audio files after a legal fight over their disclosure (NBC).

Strongest excluded candidates: for WSJ, "Nvidia Leads Defense of Open-Source AI With New Cybersecurity Initiative" — a genuine industry-shaping story, but passed over to keep category breadth, along with two other strong Iran-adjacent stories ("One Megabuyer — China — Finds a Way to Get Mideast Oil" and "Saudi Arabia Blames Iran-Backed Militias for Attack on Oil Facilities") that were judged redundant with the Caspian Sea item already selected. For NYT, "The Chips Rout Goes Global" — a real AI-chip-stocks selloff story, but judged closer to day-to-day market volatility than the institutional and infrastructure stories selected. For NBC, "Washington says goodbye to Sen. Lindsey Graham" — genuine political news, but more ceremonial than the accountability- and policy-consequential stories chosen. For AP, "Firefighters say they've never seen anything like Spain's record blaze" — a strong climate-disaster story, but redundant with the Japan earthquake already filling the natural-disaster slot and narrower in global consequence than the Afghanistan-Pakistan civilian-toll story.

No single outlet's five selections contained three or more items concerning the same underlying development; each outlet's Iran-war-adjacent or Japan-earthquake-adjacent items (never more than two per outlet) represent distinct consequences (e.g., WSJ's Caspian Sea item covers military-supply-line escalation, distinct from its earthquake and mail-ballot items). The Japan earthquake and the SCOTUS mail-ballot story each appear once in all four outlets' top five, reflecting independent judgments of their significance across the entire candidate pool rather than any single outlet's internal redundancy.
