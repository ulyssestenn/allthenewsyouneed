## Note

Selection window: 2026-08-09T13:59 UTC through 2026-08-10T13:59 UTC (09:59 ET to 09:59 ET). All eight WSJ Dow Jones RSS feeds, all ten NYT section RSS feeds, all eight NBC section RSS feeds, and the AP-focused Google News search feed listed in the source instructions returned HTTP 200 via direct fetch and were used to build each outlet's initial candidate pool (364 raw / 54 in-window for WSJ; 314 raw / 70 in-window for NYT; 177 raw / 19 in-window for NBC); items were filtered on each item's own publication timestamp (RSS `pubDate`/`published`, or, for NYT, the date embedded in the article's URL when it conflicted with a later feed-refresh timestamp — several NYT feed entries, including a recycled Amazon-data-center story already used in yesterday's digest, carried refreshed `pubDate`s on originally older articles and were excluded on that basis), not on feed-retrieval time.

Two access limitations affected sourcing, both consistent with this repository's established practice. First, NBC's own RSS feeds proved unusually thin this cycle (only 19 in-window items across all eight section feeds, dominated by shopping and lifestyle video content), so NBC's candidate pool was supplemented by directly fetching nbcnews.com's homepage and extracting article links and headlines from its server-rendered HTML, then verifying each candidate's `datePublished` via the article page's embedded metadata. One otherwise-strong candidate (Zelensky's statement that up to 50,000 North Korean troops will be deployed in Russia) was excluded after verification showed it published at 13:45 UTC on 2026-08-09, 14 minutes before this window opened. Second, apnews.com has no general-news RSS feed and its `/*.rss` paths are disallowed by robots.txt, so, as in prior runs, the Google News `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed was used for discovery; because Google News's redirect links resolve only via client-side JavaScript that a plain HTTP fetch cannot execute, and a large share of that feed's entries were bare topic-page redirects, AP's candidate pool was instead built by directly fetching apnews.com's homepage (robots.txt permits direct fetch for a generic user agent, only disallowing the `ClaudeBot`/`CCBot` crawlers by name) and extracting canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URLs and headlines from its server-rendered HTML, with each candidate's `datePublished` verified via the article page's own embedded metadata before selection. This is a direct-fetch substitution, not a fabricated or guessed URL. wsj.com and nytimes.com article pages returned 401/403 bot-detection errors on direct fetch, so WSJ and NYT selections rely on each outlet's own RSS `title`/`link`/`pubDate` fields, used verbatim; one WSJ candidate (the Will Scharf appointment) was dropped in favor of an alternate because its only in-window RSS listing carried a social-teaser sentence rather than a clean headline.
