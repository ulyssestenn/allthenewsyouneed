## Note

**Selection window:** Original publication between 2026-11-25 14:39 UTC and 2026-11-26 14:39 UTC.

**Access and verification:** All WSJ (Dow Jones), NYT, and NBC RSS feeds listed in the sourcing instructions were reachable directly. AP has no general RSS feed; AP candidates were sourced via the Google News `site:apnews.com when:1d` search feed. Each selected story's canonical `apnews.com` URL was resolved from Google's redirect wrapper using a headless-browser fetch (a direct HTTP fetch of the wrapped link returns a 302 back to itself rather than the publisher URL, since the redirect requires JS execution). A subsequent direct fetch of the resolved `apnews.com` pages to confirm exact AP headline wording was blocked by a Cloudflare "Just a moment..." interstitial, so AP headline text is rendered from the Google News feed's listing title (with the trailing "- AP News" suffix removed) rather than a scrape of the live page; the underlying facts and canonical URLs were independently cross-verified against the corresponding WSJ/NYT/NBC coverage of the same stories where overlap existed (the FCC/ABC ruling and the Lukoil asset sale).

**Principal selection rationale:** The closest thing to a single dominant story this cycle was a federal judge blocking the FCC from an early license review of Disney's ABC stations — a concrete press-freedom and regulatory-overreach ruling that led or near-led all four outlets, resolving (for now) a fight the network said had already had a "chilling effect" on its editorial decisions. Beyond that, selections favor resolved, concrete developments with durable institutional consequences: a federal consortium's completed acquisition of Lukoil's foreign assets (with The Times's angle specifically excluding Trump-administration-linked buyers), a court-ordered settlement locking in the border wall for decades, newly surfaced FBI-interview evidence that the CIA waterboarded more detainees than previously admitted, Nvidia halting Southeast Asia sales as Washington tightens chip-export restrictions on China, continuing scientific and governmental scrutiny of the Army's lead-explosives detonation in Iowa, a court win for NYC's rent freeze, SpaceX's single-million-satellite space-infrastructure approval, an escalating Ebola outbreak compounded by violence against responders, North Korea's rejection of a U.S. overture in favor of more weapons production, and the first concrete retaliation from U.S. trading partners against the tariffs announced a day earlier. The original tariff-announcement story itself — already the lead story across multiple outlets in yesterday's digest — was deliberately underweighted here in favor of these newer follow-on consequences (retaliation, export-control fallout). Also deprioritized: Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade postponement, despite being covered heavily by all four outlets, as a weather-driven one-off without durable institutional consequence, and the Aaron Rodgers–Steelers split, as a personality-driven conflict story.

**Strongest excluded candidates:**
- **WSJ:** "Altria Considers $157 Billion Tobacco Tie-Up With Imperial Brands" — a very large potential merger, but a preliminary approach rather than a concluded deal, and less immediately consequential than the selected five.
- **NYT:** "Judges in Israel Suggest Dropping Key Corruption Case Against Netanyahu" — a genuinely significant geopolitical/legal development, narrowly excluded to keep the five selections topically distinct rather than carrying two Netanyahu-adjacent items (this and the separate opposition-ousting story).
- **NBC:** "Small-town rural pharmacies in jeopardy over prescription drug costs and reimbursements" — a substantive healthcare-access story, but published only as a video segment without an accompanying text article, and the China purge story offered stronger topical diversity for the fifth slot.
- **AP:** "FCC says SpaceX can deploy 1 million satellites for data centers in space. Scientists are skeptical" — a strong infrastructure/space-policy story in its own right, excluded from AP's five because it would have duplicated ground already covered by WSJ's SpaceX pick, in favor of preserving AP's topical breadth across media law, sanctions, public health, and security.

**Redundancy check:** No outlet's final five contains three or more selections concerning the same underlying development. The FCC/ABC license ruling appears as a top story across all four outlets, and the Lukoil asset sale appears in three (WSJ, NYT, AP) — both reflect independent, genuinely shared significance (a resolved press-freedom precedent and a major sanctions-related ownership transfer) rather than redundant selection within any single outlet's list.
