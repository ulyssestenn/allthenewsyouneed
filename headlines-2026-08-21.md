## Note

**Selection window:** 2026-08-20 13:36 UTC to 2026-08-21 13:36 UTC (24 hours), filtered by original publication timestamp (each outlet's RSS `<pubDate>`, or AP's own `article:published_time` page metadata), not update or retrieval time.

**Access notes:**
- All eight listed WSJ feeds, all ten listed NYT feeds, and all eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly and parsed successfully (873 raw items across the three outlets before windowing/dedup).
- AP does not publish a general RSS feed. The Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`) was fetched for candidate discovery, but as in prior days its `<pubDate>` values reflect Google's crawl/discovery time rather than AP's original publication time (nearly all 100 results nominally fell "in window" by that timestamp alone, which is not credible), and its article links are opaque redirect tokens rather than direct `apnews.com` URLs. Instead, AP's own section front pages (`/world-news`, `/us-news`, `/politics`, `/business`, `/technology`, `/science`, `/health`, `/entertainment`, `/sports`) were fetched directly with a browser user agent (all returned HTTP 200), yielding 485 unique candidate article URLs. Each shortlisted candidate was then verified individually by reading its own `article:published_time` meta tag (not the shared "latest news" ticker timestamps used on the section fronts, which prior days' notes found unreliable). This surfaced several prominent, front-of-section stories that were not actually new: "The US national debt now stands at $40 trillion" (published 2026-08-19T21:14, already AP's own top story yesterday), "OpenAI introduces ChatGPT for Teens" (2026-08-18), "Shares in Chinese humanoid robot maker Unitree soar" (2026-08-19), and a recirculated "Takeaways from Tuesday's primaries" piece whose `article:published_time` was 2026-05-19 despite prominent placement today — all were dropped.
- WSJ and NYT article pages return 401/403 to non-browser HTTP requests even though their RSS feeds are open, so WSJ/NYT links above are used verbatim from each outlet's own RSS `<link>` field (WSJ links had tracking query parameters stripped to their canonical form), consistent with each feed's own headline text.
- One NYT candidate, "U.S.S. Lincoln Begins 13,000-Mile Journey Home, and the Shortage That Could Take Down the Grid," was excluded from consideration despite strong subject matter because its link resolves to a podcast-episode page (`/podcasts/the-headlines/`) bundling two unrelated stories under one title rather than a dedicated article.
- Two NBC picks ("USS Lincoln heads home..." aside, which has a normal text article) link to `nbcnews.com/video/` pages rather than text articles: "China hosts second Humanoid Robot Games as arms race with U.S. heats up." No text article on this specific beat appeared in the eight NBC feeds within the window; NBC's dedicated text article on China's humanoid-robot/Unitree boom ("China's backflipping robots leap to blockbuster stock market debut") published 2026-08-19, one day outside the window, so the in-window video segment was used instead.
- A New York Times article, "China Sentences Evergrande Founder to Life in Prison" (`nytimes.com/2026/08/20/business/evergrande-founder-life-prison.html`), carried an in-window `<pubDate>` today but is the identical URL already published in yesterday's digest (2026-08-20) as the same headline; its feed timestamp appears to reflect a later republish/update rather than original publication, so it was excluded as not a new development. The same applies to a WSJ opinion piece, "How the U.S. Went $40 Trillion in Debt," which is commentary on a milestone already reported and selected yesterday.

**Selection rationale:** Two federal law-enforcement stories broke overnight and dominate today's US-focused coverage: the FBI's arrest of a woman accused of an ISIS-inspired plot to bomb the New York State Capitol (a thwarted act of domestic terrorism, covered independently by NYT, NBC, and AP), and a Justice Department effort under the Trump administration to interview investigators tied to the original 2022 Mar-a-Lago documents search — a significant institutional story about the department turning its scrutiny on Trump's perceived opponents. Iran remains the dominant international throughline: Trump's threatened "economic D-Day" sanctions campaign drew a defiant response from Tehran, and AP's version of that story led its own list. Elsewhere, distinct durable developments landed across categories: Hong Kong convicted two Tiananmen-vigil organizers under its national security law (WSJ and AP both selected this, reflecting genuine significance for civil liberties in Hong Kong, not redundancy); Meta faced fresh criticism over child-safety practices amid an ongoing product-liability trial; a bipartisan political backlash against AI data centers gathered force as WSJ reported politicians in both parties turning against projects they once championed; the Trump administration moved to strip the American Bar Association of its power to accredit law schools; Italy's Monte dei Paschi launched a $40 billion bid to consolidate Italian banking; Big Tech's AI-driven borrowing binge was identified as a driver of rising bond yields; and a charter plane crash near a remote Alaska military radar site killed all 8 aboard.
