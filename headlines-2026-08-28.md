## Note

**Selection window:** 2026-08-27 13:38 UTC to 2026-08-28 13:38 UTC (24 hours), filtered by original publication timestamp (each outlet's RSS `<pubDate>`/`published`, or, for AP, each shortlisted article's own `datePublished` JSON-LD page metadata), not update or retrieval time.

**Access notes:**
- All eight listed WSJ feeds, all ten listed NYT feeds, and all eight listed NBC feeds were fetched directly and parsed successfully (888 raw items across WSJ/NYT/NBC; 273 fell inside the 24-hour window before deduplication). No domain or feed was inaccessible.
- AP does not publish a general RSS feed. The Google News search (`site:apnews.com when:1d`) was fetched for candidate discovery. Its article links are opaque `news.google.com/rss/articles/...` redirect tokens that resolve the true destination client-side via JavaScript rather than in the HTTP response, so they cannot be resolved to `apnews.com` by direct request (`curl -L` lands on the same redirect-token URL). Unlike prior days, `apnews.com` itself was directly reachable with a browser-style user agent, so each shortlisted AP candidate was matched by headline and timing to its canonical `apnews.com/article/...` URL via AP's own hub pages (`/hub/norway`, `/hub/nepal`, `/hub/bosnia-and-herzegovina`, `/hub/technology`, `/hub/epidemics`) and on-site search, then confirmed against that page's own `datePublished` JSON-LD metadata.
- Several Google News AP candidates were dropped after their canonical page's own `datePublished` proved to fall outside the window even though the story surfaced in a `when:1d` search: "Ratko Mladic, ex-Bosnian Serb warlord who oversaw atrocities, dies as a prisoner at 84" (AP's own `datePublished` 2026-08-27 13:19 UTC, 19 minutes before this window opened — NYT's own in-window RSS `pubDate` for its Mladic obituary was used instead, so the story still appears via NYT), "How Trump's goals for the Iran war have shifted over 6 months" (AP `datePublished` 2026-08-27 04:09 UTC; a distinct, later-published AP piece on the same six-month mark — "The Iran war has changed the Mideast, but not in the way the US and Israel had hoped," `datePublished` 2026-08-28 04:14 UTC — was substituted instead), "Time limits and a midnight cutoff: Meta's new rules for kids go further" (AP `datePublished` 2026-08-27 04:07 UTC), and "Exclusive: US Patriot missile stocks in Europe are 'beyond critical' due to Iran war" (no matching current AP article could be located; the closest-titled AP piece found was a March story on an unrelated Patriot-missile redeployment).
- WSJ and NYT article pages return 401/403 to non-browser HTTP requests even though their RSS feeds are open, so WSJ/NYT links above are used verbatim from each outlet's own RSS `<link>` field (WSJ links had tracking query parameters, e.g. `?mod=rss_worldnews`, stripped to their canonical form), consistent with each feed's own headline text.

**Selection rationale:** The catastrophic Nepal-Tibet glacier-collapse flood, now in its second day, again dominated the cycle and was independently selected by all four outlets, each covering a distinct facet: WSJ the overall rising death toll, NYT the fresh fear of a second flood from an unstable dammed lake threatening rescue efforts, NBC's own video-led rescue-operations reporting amid renewed rain, and AP the updated confirmed toll surpassing 500 dead. A landmark First Amendment ruling — a federal judge finding the Trump administration's blacklisting of AI company Anthropic unlawful — was selected by both WSJ and NYT as a significant precedent for government-AI industry relations. CIA Director John Ratcliffe's secret Moscow trip produced two distinct angles: WSJ's finding that senior White House aides were kept in the dark about it (a governance/process story) and NYT's reporting on the bleak intelligence assessment of Russia's war that Ratcliffe delivered there (a substantive war-outlook story) — kept separately because each reveals a different fact about the same trip, not a restatement. Ongoing litigation over Trump-era mail-in ballot rules cleared the bar for both WSJ and NBC as a live governance dispute with direct midterm-election stakes. Norway's King Harald V died at 89, with his son's accession as King Haakon VIII marking a rare monarchical succession in a G7-adjacent nation (AP); Ratko Mladic, the last major figure of the Bosnian genocide era, died in custody, closing a chapter of post-Yugoslav war-crimes accountability (NYT). Six months into the Iran war, both NYT and AP ran retrospective assessments — independently reported, but converging on the same theme of unresolved U.S. goals and an unstable regional outcome. Beyond these: WSJ's report that the U.S. is in advanced talks for an equity stake in Venezuelan oil fields, a notable precedent in state-directed energy diplomacy; NBC's coverage of a Wyoming Republican governor's objection to "aggressive" DOJ election monitors and a Kennedy Center federal judge's skeptical questioning of the administration's renaming push, both live governance disputes; AP's report of UEFA preparing a criminal complaint against FIFA's Gianni Infantino over the World Cup sell-off, a rare institutional-corruption story in global soccer with governance consequences for the sport; and AP's report that Congo's Ebola outbreak has spread to two new health zones, a global public-health story with a wire-service baseline audience. Deprioritized across outlets: Trump's executive order renaming Lake Ontario "Lake America" (widely covered but functions as political theater/stunt rather than a durable policy development), the Lindsay Clancy murder trial (crime-of-the-day, jury still deliberating with no verdict in-window), the Tate brothers' bail fight (celebrity/crime spectacle), and Selena Gomez's fraud-lawsuit dismissal request (celebrity litigation).
